CANDI on the ENCODE Imputation Challenge

In the 2019 ENCODE Imputation Challenge (EIC), 23 teams predicted 51 held-out epigenomic tracks. After the challenge, the organizers found that the held-out truth had been processed differently from the training data. They then published the scores again on two corrected versions of the truth. This board shows the published field on all three versions, scored exactly as the organizers scored it, with CANDI added as a 26th entry.

Where each entry's error comes from

Each bar is an entry's squared error on the selected layer, as a fraction of the truth's variance, so 1.0 equals predicting each track's mean. The bar splits exactly into three parts. Calibration: removed by the best monotone rescaling of the prediction. Shared: the same mistake at the same position in every held-out cell type of an assay. Specific: the rest, particular to one track.

Rows follow the board order. The board ranks entries over 9 measures, so the bar lengths need not follow it. The unit is asinh of the signal. The truth has no replicates, so no part of the error is marked as noise.

Method

The three layers
1 · Original truth
The raw predictions, scored against the blind truth as released in 2019. The blind tracks were processed as paired-end, while 90% of the training tracks were single-end. This changes duplicate removal, and so the signal. The organizers published these scores as Additional File 2 (MOESM2).
2 · Quantile normalized
The same truth, with no reprocessing. Each truth track is mapped onto one shared distribution per assay, separately inside its peaks (1,000 levels) and in the background (50 levels). The reference is the mean over the 2019 training and validation tracks. Additional File 3 (MOESM3).
3 · Reprocessed + normalized
48 truth tracks reprocessed from the reads as single-end (ATAC-seq must stay paired-end), then normalized as in layer 2. This is the organizers' corrected board, Additional File 4 (MOESM4).
How the score and the position are computed
  1. For each experiment, bootstrap and measure, the entries are ranked.
  2. Ranks are averaged over the 9 measures and normalized by the number of entries.
  3. An entry's score in a bootstrap is its mean over the 51 experiments, each capped at 0.5. Lower is better.
  4. An entry's position comes from its second-best bootstrap; ties are broken by the mean score.

The score is a rank, so it moves with the field and hides how close two entries are. Always quote the margin next to the position.

The 9 measures are genome-wide MSE, Pearson and Spearman correlation; MSE in promoters, gene bodies and enhancers; MSE weighted by the variance across cell types; and MSE at the top 1% of bins by observed (mse1obs) and by imputed (mse1imp) signal.

Reproduction, and where it follows the code over the paper

Every number here comes from Max Libbrecht's reproduction of the challenge scoring. His scorer reproduces the organizers' published tables:

  • Layer 1: every published value, to a median relative difference of 3 × 10−10; all 25 positions.
  • Layer 2: all 25 positions. Values are within a median of 2 × 10−4; the truth was rebuilt with ENCODE peak files, and DNase-seq is 30% off.
  • Layer 3: every published value, to a median relative difference of 3 × 10−10; all 25 positions. The truth is Jacob Schreiber's Zenodo records.

Where the challenge's code and the paper's Methods disagree, this board follows the code:

  • The 10 bootstraps are 10 fixed groups of chromosomes, not resampled positions.
  • Blacklisted bins are deleted before scoring.
  • Gene annotation is GENCODE v29 (the paper says v38).
  • The weights of the variance-weighted MSE are the variance across cell types of the scored truth version itself (the paper says the training set).

Each CANDI arm is added alone to the published field and the field is re-ranked, so each CANDI row is a position out of 26. The 25 published entries keep their published scores and positions. Two CANDI arms never share one field.

How the error is decomposed

Per track, the squared error divided by the truth's variance is split into an offset, a scale and a nonlinear term, which the best monotone map of the prediction would remove (shown together as calibration), and a residual that needs bins to be reordered. Across the held-out tracks of one assay, the residual splits exactly into a part shared by every track at a position and a track-specific part. Tracks are averaged with equal weight over the 51 experiments. The method is Max Libbrecht's sprint 06 decomposition, run unchanged on all 27 entries and the three truth layers. UIOWA Michaelson Lab has 50 tracks, because it submitted none for one experiment.

What "CANDI" and "CANDI + tcfloor" are
  • CANDI: the 8-assay CANDI model (about 3.1 M parameters), three seeds averaged, given each blind experiment's own recorded covariates.
  • CANDI + tcfloor: CANDI's prediction C passed through one linear map per assay, a + b·C + c·A, where A is the Average baseline. The 24 coefficients are fitted on the challenge's training tracks only, so this entry could have been submitted in 2019. For H3K4me3 and ATAC-seq the map returns almost only the Average baseline.
  • Depth-prompt variants (toggle above): the same arms with the depth covariate fixed at 30 M reads.

The organizers warn that the corrected boards should not be used to declare winners, because the teams could not retrain on the corrected data.
