% ------------------------------------------------------------------ the words --
\newcommand{\PosterTitle}{Self-supervised confidence-aware denoising imputation of genomic data}
\newcommand{\PosterAuthors}{Mehdi Foroozandeh, Abdul Rahman Diab, and Maxwell Libbrecht}
\newcommand{\PosterURL}{https://mehdiforoozandeh.github.io/CANDI/}

\newcommand{\TxtProblem}{%
Most (cell type, assay) experiments were never run, and those that were differ in
depth, read length, run type and platform. In the ENCODE Imputation Challenge, a
baseline that averages each assay over other cell types beat all but two submitted
methods~[1].}

\newcommand{\TxtCandi}{%
\textbf{C}onfidence-\textbf{A}ware \textbf{N}eural \textbf{D}enoising
\textbf{I}mputer~[2] is a self-supervised transformer over raw read counts, DNA
sequence and four experimental covariates. It predicts a distribution for every
assay in every 25\,bp bin, so each imputed value has an interval. It has no
cell-type embedding, so it runs on cell types it has never seen.}

\newcommand{\TxtFindings}{%
\begin{itemize}
  \item CANDI is \textbf{1st of 26} on the ENCODE Imputation Challenge blind test.
  \item CANDI's intervals are calibrated, and its latent features are predictive
        of gene expression.
\end{itemize}}

\newcommand{\HeadStrip}{CANDI schematic workflow}
\newcommand{\CapStrip}{%
\textbf{Top:} one cell type is sliced out of the cell type $\times$ assay $\times$
genome tensor, and a 30\,kb window of it goes into CANDI as raw counts, DNA and
covariates. Grey assays were never measured; yellow ones are masked in training.
\textbf{Bottom:} CANDI returns a mean and a 95\% interval for every assay, and
window by window this fills every missing thread of the tensor.}

\newcommand{\HeadUtility}{Downstream utility of CANDI}
\newcommand{\CapA}{%
\textbf{A}\enspace For DNase-seq (cell line DND-41), the observed coverage of
CANDI's intervals follows their stated confidence over the full range.}
\newcommand{\CapB}{%
\textbf{B}\enspace Gene expression predicted from each feature set as assays are
removed. Latent features hold $r \approx 0.79$ from 13 assays down to 4; observed
data falls to 0.60.}

\newcommand{\HeadEIC}{Fixing the signal-level shift in the ENCODE Imputation Challenge blind test}
\newcommand{\TxtEIC}{%
\textbf{The shift.} The blind test holds 51 experiments (12 cell types, 8 assays),
all of them paired-end; 85\% of the training experiments are single-end. The
blind-test tracks also sit at a different signal level from the training tracks,
by an assay-specific factor: 3.3$\times$ for H3K4me3, 1.7$\times$ for H3K27ac and
0.9--1.0$\times$ for broad marks. A model fitted to the training tracks therefore
predicts the wrong level, and CANDI's raw output ranks 14th.\par\vspace{0.2in}
\textbf{The fix.} For each assay, CANDI's output passes through one layer,
\par\vspace{0.12in}
\centerline{$\max(\beta_0 + \beta_1\cdot\text{CANDI} + \beta_2\cdot\text{baseline},\,0)$,}
\vspace{0.12in}
whose coefficients are fitted by least squares on the 133 training experiments
only. With it, CANDI ranks 1st. Our scorer reproduces every published entry to a median relative
difference of $9\times10^{-10}$.}
\newcommand{\CapC}{%
\textbf{C}\enspace The challenge's leaderboard with CANDI added. CANDI is first at
0.179 (bootstrap range 0.177--0.184); the next entry scores 0.306. Bars span the ten
bootstrap groups.}
\newcommand{\CapD}{%
\textbf{D}\enspace Each of the nine measures relative to the baseline, median over
the 51 experiments. CANDI is first on genome-wide MSE, Pearson and Spearman, and in
the top 8 on five more.}

assessment of methods for cross-cell type imputation of epigenomic profiles.

[2]~Foroozandeh M, Diab AR, Libbrecht M. CANDI: self-supervised, confidence-aware
