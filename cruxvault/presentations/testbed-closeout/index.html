covariate artifact correction (CAC) testbed · close-out

Do the recorded covariates tell us how to turn one measurement of a track into another?

Each claim carries the PI's reading and its confidence, as recorded in the vault. Most of the work was exploratory; the final three experiments were pre-registered (their rules fixed in writing before any run).

This deck closes the covariate artifact correction testbed, the final experiments included. It goes from why we built the testbed, through what it taught, to the three final experiments and what goes into the mini-CANDI prototype. For each goal it says if the goal is fully met, partially met, or not met but answered well enough to stop. Every number is read from the vault when the deck is built.

Motivationa benchmark broken by processing

In the ENCODE Imputation Challenge, one processing difference let a plain average beat almost every model

  • The cause was a distribution shift (training and test data differ systematically): the older training data were single-end, the newer blind test data paired-end, and the duplicate-removal rule differs between the two. After the correction, more than half the entries beat the baseline.
  • CANDI records such processing choices as covariates. This testbed asks if a model can use them to undo such a shift.

The challenge scored imputation models on experiments collected after the submissions closed. Under the measures fixed before the challenge, the average-activity baseline won against almost everyone. The retrospective traced this to a small difference in duplicate removal between single-end and paired-end data, not to data quality. That is exactly the kind of processing artifact that covariate correction should remove.

Motivationwhy a testbed

CANDI cannot test its covariate conditioning on its own training data

  • In CANDI, depth enters as a fixed offset: the told-depth slope is . Depth is set, not learned.
  • Told a new run type, CANDI moves its output in the right direction with a score of , which is near chance (a score of 0.5).
  • CANDI's corpora are built from released signal files, with no reads recorded. So from the corpus as built, only depth pairs can be made; every other knob needs its raw reads re-processed.

CANDI only ever pairs an experiment with a thinned copy of itself. So only depth changes between its input and its target. We cannot ask CANDI what a different read length would do, because it never saw that pair. The testbed builds those pairs on purpose.

Motivationthe question and the goals

Pre-registered (fixed in writing before any run): the competitor is a covariate-free monotone map on the value axis (the one-warp), not a blank baseline; no genome-wide correlation is a headline; every number quotes its noise floor.

The anchor question is whether the recorded covariates carry enough information to map one measurement of a track onto another measurement of the same material. Each box under it is one goal. The colour of each box is its final status, which the scorecard near the end repeats with one line of evidence.

Methodthe counterfactual arms

Each track is re-processed with one processing knob changed at a time

Each arm is the same biological material as its base. Only one processing choice differs. Read-side knobs are re-run from the raw reads or the alignments. Control-side knobs change only the control or the peak caller, so they move the minus log ten p track and leave the counts unchanged.

Methodthe model

A small network g reads both covariate sets and sets the transformation f

Design A

An affine map per bin (a scale and a shift).

Design B

A monotone curve per bin (a fixed set of knots).

Design C

A smoothing kernel, then a monotone curve.

Design D

A small convolutional network, set by g through FiLM (a per-channel scale and shift).

The covariates C describe how the input track was made. C prime describes the track we want. The model must learn the relation between them, not one map per pair of settings. Each design runs with one g per track and with one g across all tracks, each with three seeds.

Methodreferences and scoring

Every model is placed between a covariate-free map and an oracle

CRPS (continuous ranked probability score; lower is better) is always split into its oracle-scaled part (shape) and its scale-error part (level). A gain is statistically significant only when it is above 2 × the seed range (largest minus smallest of three seeds). Smaller gains stay on the slide, labelled.

The one-warp is the covariate-free monotone map. The no-covariates twin is the same model trained with no covariates. The oracle is the best any predictor can do. For a depth pair it is the exact binomial thinning law, because a depth arm is a random subset of the base's reads. Trained pairs are base to arm; never-trained pairs are arm to arm, which training never showed.

Results(a) the covariates against the no-covariates twin

The covariates help on trained pairs; most of the help is lost on never-trained pairs

The bars are relative gains over the twin, as a median over the design and g configurations. Trained pairs are base to arm. Never-trained pairs are arm to arm, with one knob or two knobs changed. The drop is largest in the minus log ten p space.

Results(b) depth, against the one-warp and the exact oracle

On depth pairs, designs B to D close most of the gap from the one-warp to the oracle

Gap closed is the one-warp error minus the model error, over the one-warp error minus the oracle error. One is the oracle; zero is the one-warp. The whisker is the seed noise in the same units. The PI signed the claim as supported for designs B to D. Design A is the exception.

Results(c) run type, against the one-warp

On run-type pairs, the model beats the one-warp on histone marks; DNase is mixed

The paired-end arm is a separate re-processing, not a subset of the base's reads. So no exact oracle exists, and the independent-replicate floor sits above the one-warp. Gap closed cannot be read here; the gain over the one-warp can. The PI accepted the bar of beating the one-warp by more than two times the seed noise, with no oracle; the reading stays weak because that bar was set after the run.

Results(d) capacity of f and a depth offset

A depth offset repairs design A's scale error; nothing in f fixes never-trained pairs

The offset removes the source depth before f and adds the target depth after it. It helps the affine design, mostly in scale error. The other designs do not clear the criterion. More capacity in f leaves the never-trained gain where it was, so the limit is in g, not in the form of f.

Results(e) the depth law on never-trained pairs

Only the chain through the base meets the depth law on never-trained pairs

The depth law says that a track at half the depth has half the counts. The real arms obey it closely. Every direct design learns it on trained pairs and misses it on never-trained arm to arm pairs. Removing the pseudocount, changing the loss, making g or f wider, and an encoder-decoder did not fix it. Running two trained maps in series through the base does.

Results(f) training g on arm-to-arm pairs: the checks

The first arm adds two arm to arm depth pairs per track to training. The second arm adds every arm to arm pair that does not touch the held-out depth. The checks were pre-registered and approved by the PI before the run. Both arms finished every run. Both controls pass, so the rule reading is final: it fails, on the CRPS check. The PI signed it as not supported: arm to arm training teaches the scale of the answer, not its distribution.

Results(g) training g on arm-to-arm pairs: CRPS on held-out pairs

Each bar is a CRPS split into its shape part and its scale-error part. The whisker marks the seed range. The DNase class has one track, so its pairs are few and its seed range is wide. The pre-registered rule reads only the first arm. The second arm is complete; its bars are a reading, not a check.

Results(h) from counts to the p-value track

Predict counts, then derive the p-value track by the peak caller's formula

The direct designs that predict the p-value track sometimes explode on sparse to full depth pairs. A separate map h, from a predicted count distribution and its control to the p-value, does not explode. The peak caller's own formula does most of the work. Training h on f's predictions does not repair f's errors. By PI ruling, the redesigned model has no direct p-value head: it predicts counts, and the p-value track comes from the formula.

Results(i) which knobs move a track

Every knob moves peak height far above the floor; few move the shape above it

Peak height is the log two change of the top one percent of bins on one shared peak set. Shape is one minus the rank correlation at one kilobase. The floor is the distance between two independent half libraries at the arm's depth. A knob arm reuses the base's reads, so below the floor does not mean no effect.

Results(j) how much resampling alone moves a track

Resampling noise shrinks with depth, and signal level sets it, not region

The floor is the distance between the two pseudoreplicate halves of a product, thinned along a depth ladder. The first pass thinned each bin alone and split reads, which made the one kilobase shape too smooth. The rebuild drops whole reads, and it matches the real depth-arm halves. The value at the full depth is an extrapolation.

Results(k) where along the genome each knob acts

Depth acts the same everywhere; mapping quality and read length act where mappability is low

The residual is what the one-warp leaves per one kilobase bin. Signal level is fitted first. The feature gain is the extra variance that GC, mappability, repeats and peak context explain together. The shuffle control permutes the feature rows. The depth arm sits on its thinning null, so depth needs no position-aware correction.

Results(l) a trained CANDI, told a counterfactual covariate

Told a new read length or run type, CANDI moves its level, but not the way the real tracks move

The probe told a trained CANDI checkpoint an arm's read length or run type in place of the base's, and compared the predicted change with the real arm. The PI's reading: CANDI uses these covariates in the wrong direction. This describes CANDI as it is now, not the testbed runs. Missing counterfactual pairs fit this, but low conditional information and collinearity of the covariates are joint causes. Whether it moves the right way per region could not be tested, because the positive control failed, so the probe is parked.

Methodthe final experiments

Three final tests on one correction model shared across the tracks, in count space

(a) A joint objective

Does adding cross-assay imputation or masked-bin denoising (predict hidden bins of the same track) change transfer to never-trained pairs?

(b) A pivot through the base

Does routing arm to base to arm, with one shared model, beat the direct route on every count-moving knob?

(c) A mappability input

Does a per-bin mappability channel (how uniquely reads align there) help the read-length and mapping-quality arms?

All three experiments use design D, one model shared across the seven tracks, trained on base to arm pairs and scored on never-trained arm to arm pairs on held-out chromosomes. The first tests if the testbed's negatives on model form survive joint training, which CANDI does. The second tests if the routing fix of the depth law carries to a shared model and to every knob. The third tests if a position-aware input fixes the knobs that act through mappability.

Methodpre-registration

Every check was fixed before any run; the repairs after the smoke test came before the full runs

A smoke test is a short single-seed run that proves the pipeline works. A difference counts only when it is above 2 × the larger seed range (maximum minus minimum over seeds) of the two arms compared, unless the experiment sets another rule. Statistical power is the chance to detect a true effect of the expected size.

The checks were drafted by a pre-run reviewer that did not see the argument for each claim, and the PI approved each one in chat before any run. After the smoke test, the PI made repairs, all recorded in each hypothesis: among them a scrambled-covariate twin for the joint arms, an agreement loss in place of a consistency loss that collapsed, a no-covariates floor for the pivot, and new mappability strata, because many blocks are fully mappable.

Results(m) a joint objective

Each bar is the never-trained CRPS split into its shape part and its scale-error part, with the seed range as a whisker. The right panel shows each arm minus correction alone, against the grey band of two times the seed range. Nothing leaves the band at weight one. The scrambled-covariate twins are far worse on never-trained pairs, so the joint models do use the covariates. The PI read this as against, weak.

Results(n) a pivot through the base

Each dot on the left is one never-trained cell, a source knob and a target knob. A dot above the diagonal is a cell where the route through the base wins by more than two times the seed range. The right panel pools every cell. The route through the base is lower in every seed, but by less than the bar, so it is not significant. The PI read this as against, moderate. The agreement-loss arm was not planned as a test of the direct route, so its gain is exploratory.

Results(o) a mappability input

The lowest-mappability decile is the tenth of one kilobase blocks where reads align least uniquely, with the blacklist removed; the strata were fixed before the run. The shifted control moves the mappability track by five megabases, so it keeps the channel and its parameters but breaks its alignment with the genome. It keeps almost none of the gain. The failed control is the depth arm, which should not change and improved instead. That caps the reading at weak.

Discussionwhat this rules in and out

The covariates carry the information; no correction tried carries it to unseen pairs

Ruled in

The covariates beat the no-covariates twin on trained pairs (a). On depth, designs B to D sit near the exact oracle (b). Every knob moves peak height above the floor (i). A mappability channel helps where mappability is low (o).

Ruled out

The depth-law miss is not the pseudocount, the loss, the size of g or f, or the encoder-decoder (e). Arm-to-arm training does not fix the distribution (f). A shared model gains nothing from the route through the base (n).

Located

The gap is in transfer to pairs never trained on, not in capacity. At testbed scale, joint training with imputation does not change it (m), so the testbed's negatives on model form are not an artifact of training the correction alone.

The discussion adds no number. Each card restates result slides by their letters. The joint-objective reading is weak, so the last card is a reading at testbed scale, not a claim about CANDI at full scale.

Discussionwhat goes into the mini-CANDI prototype

What goes into the mini-CANDI prototype

Count space only

Predict counts; derive the p-value track by the formula (h). No direct p-value head (PI ruling).

Depth as a learned probe

A mappability channel

No routing through a base

Costly reprocessing deferred

Mini-CANDI is a staged, first-principles prototype: few assays, few covariates, few parameters, then scale. Depth stays a learned input on purpose: its law is known exactly, so how well the network follows it is a cheap measure of how much correction it learns. The closed-form depth offset is parked for the publication version. New counterfactual pairs that need raw reads re-processed wait until their return is clear.

Discussionlimitations

Limitations

Few tracks

Low statistical power

A check that cannot fail

A failed no-change control

No oracle for re-processed arms

A re-run arm is not a subset of its base's reads, so only depth has an exact oracle. For run type, gap closed cannot be read (c).

Many readings are exploratory

Many readings in (a) to (k) were made after the run, or with a bar or an oracle set after it; each finding says which. The final experiments (m) to (o) were pre-registered.

Each limitation names what it affects. None of them reverses a reading; each one bounds how far a reading carries. The low power means a null result in the final experiments is a weak null.

Glossary: the testbed

Base, arm
A track as made by the standard pipeline; the same track with one knob changed.
One-warp
One monotone map from base values to arm values, with no covariates.
No-covariates twin
The same model, trained with the covariates removed.
Oracle
The best possible predictor; for depth, the exact binomial thinning law.
Gap closed
The share of the one-warp-to-oracle distance that a model removes.
CRPS
Continuous ranked probability score: the error of a predicted distribution.
Oracle-scaled part, scale error
The CRPS split into shape error and level error.
Seed range
Largest minus smallest value over three seeds (random restarts).
Depth law
Counts scale with sequencing depth: half the reads, half the counts.
Trained, never-trained pair
Base-to-arm pairs seen in training; arm-to-arm pairs never seen.
Through the base
Two trained maps in series: arm to base, then base to arm.
Resampling floor
How far two independent half libraries of one track differ.

Each term is also glossed on the slide where it first appears.

Glossary: the final experiments

CAC
Covariate artifact correction: undoing processing effects using the recorded covariates.
Shared correction model
One design-D model for all seven tracks, in count space.
Cross-assay imputation
Predict a masked assay of the same cell type from the others.
Masked-bin denoising
Predict hidden bins of a track from the rest of it.
Matched loss scale
Correction alone, with its loss rescaled as a joint loss would be.
Scrambled-covariate twin
The same model, covariates shuffled at training time.
Agreement loss
A penalty on disagreement between the direct and the routed predictions.
Mappability
How uniquely short reads align at a position.
Lowest-mappability decile
The tenth of one-kilobase blocks with the lowest mean mappability.
Statistical power
The chance that a test detects a true effect of the expected size.

Each term is also glossed on the slide where it first appears, or in the method slides of the final experiments.
