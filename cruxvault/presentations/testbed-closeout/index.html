This deck closes the testbed. For each goal we set at the start, it says if the goal is fully met, partially met, or not met but answered well enough to stop. The arc is motivation, method, results and discussion. Every number is read from the vault when the deck is built.

Motivationwhy a testbed

CANDI cannot test its covariate conditioning on its own training data

  • In CANDI, depth enters as a fixed offset: the told-depth slope is . Depth is set, not learned.
  • Told a new run type, CANDI moves its output in the right direction with a score of , which is near chance (a score of 0.5).

CANDI only ever pairs an experiment with a thinned copy of itself. So only depth changes between its input and its target. We cannot ask CANDI what a different read length would do, because it never saw that pair. The testbed builds those pairs on purpose.

Motivationthe question and the goals

Pre-registered (fixed in writing before any run): the competitor is a covariate-free monotone map on the value axis (the one-warp), not a blank baseline; no genome-wide correlation is a headline; every number quotes its noise floor.

The anchor question is whether the recorded covariates carry enough information to map one measurement of a track onto another measurement of the same material. Each box under it is one goal. The colour of each box is its final status, which the scorecard near the end repeats with one line of evidence.

Methodthe counterfactual arms

Each track is re-processed with one processing knob changed at a time

Each arm is the same biological material as its base. Only one processing choice differs. Read-side knobs are re-run from the raw reads or the alignments. Control-side knobs change only the control or the peak caller, so they move the minus log ten p track and leave the counts unchanged.

Methodthe model

A small network g reads both covariate sets and sets the transformation f

Design A

An affine map per bin (a scale and a shift).

Design B

A monotone curve per bin (a fixed set of knots).

Design C

A smoothing kernel, then a monotone curve.

Design D

A small convolutional network, set by g through FiLM (a per-channel scale and shift).

The covariates C describe how the input track was made. C prime describes the track we want. The model must learn the relation between them, not one map per pair of settings. Each design runs with one g per track and with one g across all tracks, each with three seeds.

Methodreferences and scoring

Every model is placed between a covariate-free map and an oracle

CRPS (continuous ranked probability score; lower is better) is always split into its oracle-scaled part (shape) and its scale-error part (level). A gain is statistically significant only when it is above 2 × the seed range (largest minus smallest of three seeds). Smaller gains stay on the slide, labelled.

The one-warp is the covariate-free monotone map. The no-covariates twin is the same model trained with no covariates. The oracle is the best any predictor can do. For a depth pair it is the exact binomial thinning law, because a depth arm is a random subset of the base's reads. Trained pairs are base to arm; never-trained pairs are arm to arm, which training never showed.

Results(a) the covariates against the no-covariates twin

The covariates help on trained pairs; most of the help is lost on never-trained pairs

The bars are relative gains over the twin, as a median over the design and g configurations. Trained pairs are base to arm. Never-trained pairs are arm to arm, with one knob or two knobs changed. The drop is largest in the minus log ten p space.

Results(b) depth, against the one-warp and the exact oracle

On depth pairs, designs B to D close most of the gap from the one-warp to the oracle

Gap closed is the one-warp error minus the model error, over the one-warp error minus the oracle error. One is the oracle; zero is the one-warp. The whisker is the seed noise in the same units. The PI has not signed this reading, and the model design for the verdict is still his choice.

Results(c) run type, against the one-warp

On run-type pairs, the model beats the one-warp on histone marks; DNase is mixed

The paired-end arm is a separate re-processing, not a subset of the base's reads. So no exact oracle exists, and the independent-replicate floor sits above the one-warp. Gap closed cannot be read here; the gain over the one-warp can. Not judged by the PI.

Results(d) capacity of f and a depth offset

A depth offset repairs design A's scale error; nothing in f fixes never-trained pairs

The offset removes the source depth before f and adds the target depth after it. It helps the affine design, mostly in scale error. The other designs do not clear the criterion. More capacity in f leaves the never-trained gain where it was, so the limit is in g, not in the form of f.

Results(e) the depth law on never-trained pairs

Only the chain through the base meets the depth law on never-trained pairs

The depth law says that a track at half the depth has half the counts. The real arms obey it closely. Every direct design learns it on trained pairs and misses it on never-trained arm to arm pairs. Removing the pseudocount, changing the loss, making g or f wider, and an encoder-decoder did not fix it. Running two trained maps in series through the base does.

Results(f) training g on arm-to-arm pairs: the checks

The first arm adds two arm to arm depth pairs per track to training. The second arm adds every arm to arm pair that does not touch the held-out depth. The checks were pre-registered and approved by the PI before the run. The first arm's readings are final. The second arm is still running, so the two controls are not yet readable and the PI has not signed.

Results(g) training g on arm-to-arm pairs: CRPS on held-out pairs

The CRPS failure is in DNase, and it is scale error

Each bar is a CRPS split into its shape part and its scale-error part. The whisker marks the seed range. The DNase class has one track, so its pairs are few and its seed range is wide. The second arm's bars are partial while it runs.

Results(h) from counts to the p-value track

A map from counts to the p-value track is stable, and is limited by the count model

The direct designs that predict the p-value track sometimes explode on sparse to full depth pairs. A separate map h, from a predicted count distribution and its control to the p-value, does not explode. Training h on f's predictions does not repair f's errors. The direct p-space fix is deferred until the count model settles, by PI ruling.

Results(i) which knobs move a track

Every knob moves peak height far above the floor; few move the shape above it

Peak height is the log two change of the top one percent of bins on one shared peak set. Shape is one minus the rank correlation at one kilobase. The floor is the distance between two independent half libraries at the arm's depth. A knob arm reuses the base's reads, so below the floor does not mean no effect.

Results(j) how much resampling alone moves a track

Resampling noise shrinks with depth, and signal level sets it, not region

The floor is the distance between the two pseudoreplicate halves of a product, thinned along a depth ladder. The first pass thinned each bin alone and split reads, which made the one kilobase shape too smooth. The rebuild drops whole reads, and it matches the real depth-arm halves. The value at the full depth is an extrapolation.

Results(k) where along the genome each knob acts

Depth acts the same everywhere; mapping quality and read length act where mappability is low

The residual is what the one-warp leaves per one kilobase bin. Signal level is fitted first. The feature gain is the extra variance that GC, mappability, repeats and peak context explain together. The shuffle control permutes the feature rows. The depth arm sits on its thinning null, so depth needs no position-aware correction.

Results(l) a trained CANDI, told a counterfactual covariate

CANDI's output moves when told a new read length or run type, but the test could not be read

The swap is wired

Prompt rows change one field in the target column only.

The depth control fails

Rows pass; told depth also moves the learned part of the output.

No pair clears the floor

Knob pairs clear the split-half reproducibility floor; that floor statistic is biased down.

The probe told a trained CANDI checkpoint an arm's read length or run type in place of the base's. The output level moves, so CANDI does not ignore these covariates. Whether it moves the right way per region could not be tested, because the positive control failed. The PI parked the probe and moved the question to arm to arm training.

Discussionwhat this rules in and out

The covariates carry the information; a direct g does not carry it to unseen pairs

Ruled in

The covariates beat the no-covariates twin on trained pairs (a). On depth, designs B to D sit near the exact oracle (b). Every knob moves peak height above the floor (i).

Ruled out

The depth-law miss is not the pseudocount, the loss, the size of g or f, or a missing encoder-decoder structure (e). More capacity in f does not help never-trained pairs (d).

Located

The gap is in how g answers a pair it never saw. Two trained maps through the base meet the law (e). Arm-to-arm training meets the law but not the CRPS (f, g).

Backupglossary

Glossary

Base, arm
A track as made by the standard pipeline; the same track with one knob changed.
One-warp
One monotone map from base values to arm values, with no covariates.
No-covariates twin
The same model, trained with the covariates removed.
Oracle
The best possible predictor; for depth, the exact binomial thinning law.
Gap closed
The share of the one-warp-to-oracle distance that a model removes.
CRPS
Continuous ranked probability score: the error of a predicted distribution.
Oracle-scaled part, scale error
The CRPS split into shape error and level error.
Seed range
Largest minus smallest value over three seeds (random restarts).
Depth law
Counts scale with sequencing depth: half the reads, half the counts.
Trained, never-trained pair
Base-to-arm pairs seen in training; arm-to-arm pairs never seen.
Through the base
Two trained maps in series: arm to base, then base to arm.
Resampling floor
How far two independent half libraries of one track differ.

Each term is also glossed on the slide where it first appears.
