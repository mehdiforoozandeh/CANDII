Phase 1 tested a symmetric encoder-decoder: one g and one CNN, used twice, through a learned "clean" middle setting. From that deck the PI picked three paths for phase 2. This deck reports them.

The deck follows the order of a paper: motivation, method, results (one slide per metric, nine metrics), discussion.

Motivationwhat phase 1 showed (4000 steps)

CRPS is the error of the whole predicted distribution, in counts; lower is better. Dark is the oracle-scaled part, light is the scale error (both explained on the method slides). Black ticks are the three seeds.

Never-trained pairs are arm to arm pairs that the model never saw in training. They are the question of this whole line of work: does the model carry its fit over to them?

Motivationthe three paths the PI picked

Three paths: fix the middle at the base, anchor the middle to the real base track, and train longer

Every route maps a source track (an arm) to a target track. The encoder-decoder routes go through a middle: the encoder maps the arm to the middle, and the same network, as the decoder, maps the middle to the target.

Learned middle: the middle setting is learned (phase 1's default). Fixed middle: the middle setting is the base track's own setting and does not move. Anchored middle: the setting is fixed at the base, and the encoder must also predict the real base track; the decoder reads that prediction. Old D maps the arm straight to the target. Old D through the base chains two of old D's trained maps: arm to base, then base to target.

The third path, more steps, applies to every arm: the cap goes from four thousand to twelve thousand steps.

Methodthe arms

Four trained arms and one reference; each arm changes one thing from the one above it

armmiddle settingthe decoder readslossesbest step chosen byruns scoredof assigned

L1, cross: encode the source, decode with the target's setting, and score the real target; and the other way round. L2, round trip: encode, decode with the track's own setting, and score the track itself. Anchor loss: the encoder's own output is a negative binomial prediction of the base track, scored against the real base track. Each loss has weight one.

Old D retrained is the t118 rung D, retrained with the twelve-thousand-step cap. Old D through the base needs no training: it reuses old D's new maps.

Every arm runs on each per-track g and on the one g across tracks, with three seeds. The 7.5M depth arm is held out of training.

Methodwhat we score, and how a run is selected

Training sees only base ↔ arm pairs; we score those, a held-out depth, and the never-trained arm → arm pairs

Panel (a): each track was rebuilt many times, changing one processing setting (a knob) at a time; each rebuild is an arm. Training uses pairs between the base and one arm, both ways. The depth arm at 7.5M reads is held out: its pairs with the base are scored, never trained. Arm to arm pairs are never trained; "same knob" pairs join two arms of one knob, "two knobs" pairs join arms of two different knobs.

Panel (b): each run keeps the step with the best validation likelihood on chromosome 22, using its own loss terms. Every score is on chromosomes 19 and 21, with the blacklist removed.

Methodhow to read every number

Every CRPS is split into a shape part and a scale part, and every gap carries a noise label

CRPS (continuous ranked probability score) compares the whole predicted distribution of each bin with the observed count. Lower is better; zero is a perfect, certain prediction. Pooled CRPS: per seed, the mean over a track's pairs, then over tracks; the reading is the mean over seeds.

Oracle-scaled part: the CRPS left after the one best rescaling of the prediction; it measures the shape. Scale error: what that rescaling removes; it measures a wrong total. The project's rule: no CRPS without its split.

Noise rule (spec): the seed spread of a cell is the largest gap between its three seeds. A gap counts only if it is larger than two times the larger seed spread of the two cells. Smaller gaps are shown and labelled "within noise".

Resultsmetric 2 · the generalisation gap

What it measuresNever-trained CRPS minus trained CRPS, same arm. The never-trained side uses same-knob pairs.
Best caseNear 0: unseen pairs are as good as trained ones.
Worst caseLarge and positive: the fit stays on the trained pairs.

Bars: the pooled gap, with seed ticks. Below zero means the never-trained pairs score better than the trained ones. The text column gives each gap with its split (oracle-scaled part, then scale error) and its noise label against old D.

Resultsmetric 2 · why every pooled gap is negative

The pooled gap is negative because the two pools hold different pairs, not because unseen pairs are easier

CRPS on counts grows with the depth of the target track: a deep target has large counts and a large error. So a pool with many deep targets has a high CRPS, whatever the model.

Panel (a): the gap per knob, one g per track, with its noise label against old D in the text. Panel (b): the make-up of the two pools. Knobs with one arm only have no same-knob pair, so they sit only in the trained pool. Half of the trained depth pairs have the base, the deepest track, as their target; the never-trained depth pairs target the shallower arms.

Worst caseAbove old D outside noise: the middle buys never-trained gains with trained-pair losses.

Resultsmetric 5 · the depth law (a sanity check)

What it measures|log2 predicted scale − log2 true read ratio| on depth pairs. Half the reads should halve every bin.
Best caseNear 0 in every pair group: the model scales counts by the read ratio.
Worst caseNear 1 or more: off by a factor of 2, as a map that returns its input would be.

Each row has four thin bars, one per pair group: trained, the held-out 7.5M, never-trained depth to depth, and never-trained pairs that touch 7.5M. Ticks are the seeds. Log scale. Old D through the base exists only for never-trained pairs.

Resultsmetric 6 · hop 1: does the middle hold the base?

What it measuresCRPS of the predicted base track, from an arm, against the real base. For arms with the middle at the base.
Best caseAs low as old D's own arm → base map: the middle is a good base track.
Worst caseWell above old D: the middle is not the base, whatever its setting says.

Fixed middle and old D: the decoder's arm to base prediction (for the fixed middle, the decoder under the base's own setting). Anchored middle: the same decoder prediction, and the encoder's own prediction of the base, which is what the decoder reads. Pairs: the trained arm to base pairs; the text adds the held-out 7.5M to base pairs.

Resultsmetric 7 · middle consistency

What it measuresFor two arms of one track: median |Z − Z′| over median |x − x′|, the middles' gap over the inputs' gap.
Best caseWell below 1: the middle removes the processing difference; one sample, one middle.
Worst case1 or more: the middles differ as much as the inputs, or more.

x is log(1 + count), the encoder's input. Z is the middle the decoder reads: for the learned and fixed middles, the encoder's Gaussian mean; for the anchored middle, log(1 + the predicted base mean), which is in the same units as x. The ratio is taken per pair of products, then the median. Pairs where two arms differ in two knobs; the line below adds pairs with the base or two arms of one knob.

Caveat: the learned and fixed middles are not in the units of x, so their ratio is a weaker reading than the anchored middle's.

Resultsmetric 8 · the swap check against the identity distance

What it measuresSwap: with c′ = c nothing should change; how far the prediction moves from X. Against how far it moves on real pairs.
Best caseSwap near 0 while the identity distance is large: bottom right.
Worst caseA large swap (it changes X for nothing), or an identity distance near 0 (it ignores the settings).

Both axes: the median, over bins with a count above zero, of |log(predicted mean ÷ X)|. Swap (y): give the model the same setting on both sides. Identity distance (x): real pairs, against the source X. Small marks: one run (median over its knobs). Large marks: the cell mean. Old D has no identity distance in the tables, so its swap value is a horizontal line.

Resultsmetric 9 · was the step cap the limit?

What it measuresThe share of runs whose best step is in the last 10 % of the 12,000-step cap.
Best case0: every run found its best step well before the cap; steps were not the limit.
Worst caseMany runs: the cap cut training short, and more steps could still help.

Each dot is one run's best step (by its own validation loss on chromosome 22); each small tick is where that run stopped. Early stopping (an evaluation every 250 steps, patience 4) ends a run after four evaluations with no gain. The lines mark the phase-1 cap of four thousand steps, the start of the last 10 %, and the cap.

Discussionwhat each metric tells us

Nine readings: the middle costs nothing and passes every check, but only old D through the base improves never-trained pairs

One line per metric, restated from the results. Tier 1 (metrics 1 and 2) is the question. Tier 2 (metrics 3 to 5) checks that nothing else got worse. Tier 3 (metrics 6 to 9) explains the first two.

Discussionwhat this says about how to formulate g and f

With one g per track, the closer the middle is to the real base track, the lower the never-trained CRPS; only the chain through the base clears the noise

The figure orders the routes by how much of the real base track the middle holds: a learned setting, the base's setting, a prediction of the base trained against the real base, and old D's chain through the predicted base. Never-trained CRPS falls along that order. Only the last step passes the noise rule, so the order is a reading, not a result.

Hop 1 says the anchored encoder already predicts the base as well as old D. What still differs from the chain through the base is the second hop: one shared network decoding a middle, against old D's own base to arm map.

Discussionnext steps · the deferred scale questions

The scale questions wait until g and f work together; these are the options, not recommendations

option
A bigger g
A wider or deeper MLP than 2 × 64. It would teach whether the settings → FiLM map limits the never-trained pairs, most of which change two knobs at once.
option
A bigger CNN
More channels or layers than 4 × 32. It would teach whether one shared network is too small to be both encoder and decoder.
option
Another activation
The CNN uses GELU now. Another activation would teach whether its shape limits how well f follows the depth law's straight scaling.

PI ruling, 2026-10-25: these wait until phase 2 defines how g and f work together. The readings above do not rank them.

These three are the spec's deferred list. Each is an option with what it would teach; none is ranked.
