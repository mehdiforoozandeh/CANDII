miniCANDI progress: first on the ENCODE Imputation Challenge validation board, and what still limits it

One external benchmark ( validation experiments, entries), then three studies of the model itself: read starts against read counts, recalibration of the predicted spread, and covariate prompts.

Motivationan external, fixed benchmark

The 2019 ENCODE Imputation Challenge lets us rank miniCANDI against published entries

predict

miniCANDI predicts the read count of each 25 bp bin as a negative binomial (count distribution with a mean and a spread).

→
convert

The predicted mean and the observed control go through the MACS2 rule (standard ChIP-seq peak caller) to a −log10 p-value track, the challenge's signal.

→
score

→
place

The organizers' combined rank (mean rank over measures) per experiment gives one board position. An unscored experiment counts as the worst.

The challenge withheld some assays in some cell types. The validation set is the round of experiments whose truth was released, and the paper's supplementary file gives every round-one entry's scores on it, so we can place a new entry without the organizers. Our model works in raw counts, so the entry needs a conversion step to the challenge's p-value signal. That conversion is fixed; nothing in it is tuned on validation truth.

Methodwhat the entry uses, and what a 2019 entrant did not have

Nothing is fitted on validation truth, but the entry uses each target's own processed reads

Fitted on training tracks only

The model weights. The read-depth cap, chosen on training ChIP-seq tracks.

No factor is tuned on validation truth.

From the target's own reads

Read depth, read length and fragment length of each target experiment, and its observed control counts. A 2019 entrant had none of these for a withheld experiment.

Disclosures

A sensitivity board with neutral covariate prompts is not run yet. interim

The middle card is the main caveat. Our model is conditioned on experimental covariates, and on this board those covariates come from the target experiment's own reads. A round-one entrant predicted a withheld experiment without them. The neutral-prompt board would measure how much of our position rests on them. Fragment length matters only for the conversion step.

Results(a) the validation board

With the depth cap, miniCANDI is first of on all validation experiments

Each row is one board. Read each group separately: the top group scores the same thirty experiments and counts the rest as worst, so it understates every entry of ours. The bottom group is the complete board. There is no seed range here: the board scores one model seed, and the only spread is over the organizers' bootstrap samples.

Results(b) rank per measure, depth-capped board

We rank best on correlation and worst on error at the bins we predict highest

Each bar is one measure, split by our rank among the ten round-one entries on each experiment. Correlations ignore scale, so they reward the right shape. The error at our own top-predicted bins punishes peaks that are too tall, which is the weakness the next slides trace.

Results(c) the read-depth cap

The 2019 truth looks like about M reads, so we cap ChIP-seq depth there

The floor is the best a perfect count model could do: we put the experiment's own observed reads through our conversion and score that against the organizers' truth. On deep experiments the floor was bad, because the organizers processed fewer reads than our store holds. Thinning to the cap was chosen on training tracks only, then applied unchanged to validation. DNase-seq keeps full depth, because no cap helped on its training tracks.

Results(d) prediction error against the conversion floor

The bottleneck is our count overshoot at peaks, not the conversion

Each point is one validation track before the depth cap. The horizontal axis is the floor, our conversion applied to the observed reads. The vertical axis is our prediction through the same conversion. Points far above the diagonal are model error. Orange points are tracks whose predicted counts are too tall for how well they correlate. The p-value transform is convex, so a count overshoot at a peak becomes a larger p-value overshoot.

Results(e) what did not help on the board

A fairly fitted peak shrink and a recalibrated spread did not help

Panel a: the peak shrink rescales each prediction toward its mean by one slope per assay. Fitted with the target's own experiment included, it helps, but that uses validation truth. Fitted with that experiment left out, the fair version, the gain mostly goes away, because the right slope varies between experiments of one assay. Panel b: recalibration widens or narrows the predicted spread, and the expected p-value then moves. On this board it made the error worse.

Results(f) read-start targets against read-count targets

Read counts beat read starts on imputation; correction is close

A read start is the five-prime end of a read, counted per bin; a read count counts every read that overlaps the bin. Panel a is why we tried read starts: with the strand, they rebuild the fragment pileup the challenge used, and overlap counts cannot. Panel b is the same recipe trained on each target, three seeds each, sixteen regions. The dots are seeds. A gap counts only when it is larger than both models' seed ranges, which is our project's noise floor.

Results(g) why read starts lost

The conversion explains the overconfidence; H3K36me3 explains most of the imputation gap

Panel a: the imputation gap is not uniform. One broad mark, which covers whole gene bodies, carries most of it, and the read-start model is even better on one acetylation mark. Smoothing the read-start prediction made that broad mark worse, so the error is a level error over long domains, not per-bin noise. Panel b: the conversion from starts to counts treats neighbouring bins as independent. Correcting for the measured correlation length removes most or all of the overconfidence. Training budget is the one cause still untested.

Results(h) recalibrating the predicted spread

For imputation, the honest recalibration gain is inside seed noise

Recalibration divides the negative binomial size by one factor per assay. It never changes the mean, so imputation and correction scores do not move; only calibration can. For an imputed track a user has no truth in that cell, so the factor must come from other cells: that is the deployment fit. The same-cell fit is an upper bound for imputation, but a user can reach it for correction, where the observed track exists.

Results(i) interval coverage at large peaks

Our intervals cover large observed peaks almost as well as a calibrated model

Coverage is the fraction of bins whose observed count falls inside the predicted interval. Picking bins by their observed value lowers coverage even for a perfect model, because the highest observed bins are mostly upper-tail draws. So the grey bars redraw each bin from its own predicted distribution and apply the same selection. The gap between teal and grey is the real miss.

Results(j) covariate prompts

Depth steers every target; an unknown lab or antibody does not widen the intervals

For each imputed target we changed one output covariate and kept the rest, and we wrote the expected direction down before the run. A bar is the share of targets that met that rule in every seed. Two rules were wrong about the form of the effect rather than the model: duplicates and mapping quality change the spread and the shape, not the total. The real gap is the unknown values, which should make the model less confident and do not.

Discussionstatus and next steps interim

Build the blind-set entry with the same pipeline once Nibi returns. The blind board is the one that counts.

Avocado baseline

Score Avocado through the same conversion, so the comparison shares one pipeline.

Covariate prompts

Design gap: unknown values should widen the intervals. Run the neutral-prompt board.

Read starts

Phase two is on hold. The one open cause, training budget, needs a longer run.

Ruled in: the depth cap, fitted without validation truth, is what lifts the board position. Ruled out: a post-hoc peak shrink or recalibration as the fix for the overshoot. The fix belongs in training. Read starts stay parked until the PI decides whether a longer run is worth it.
