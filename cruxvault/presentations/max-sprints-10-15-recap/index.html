Sprint 10 · covariate correction · the questions

Two experiments on the same cell type and assay differ. Max asked two things.

Question A
How much of the experiment-specific variation is predictable, even in principle?
→
Question B
How do we impute in its presence? The candidate was a covariate artifact correction (CAC) module, i.e. remove an experiment's own patterns at the input and add them back at the output.
→
Outcome
A has an answer with ranges. B has a partial answer. No CAC module was built.
  • Replicate: a second library of the same experiment. Second experiment: a separate ENCODE experiment on the same cell type and assay.
Sprint 10 · question A · result

About 44% of the error is noise, 26% belongs to the experiment, 28% is reachable.

Not predictable: a replicate does not reproduce itExplained by recorded covariates and depthBelongs to the individual experimentReachable by a better model
  • Recorded covariates and depth explain only 2%.
  • The parts were measured on different subsets of targets, and the noise floor comes from replicates that were not matched. Max's report rounds the noise share to 45%.
Sprint 10 · question B · the negative result

No recorded covariate predicted the experiment's part.

What was tried
  • Single-end against paired-end reads: no detectable effect across 31 pairs of experiments. Effects up to about 0.08 in level error are not excluded.
  • A corrector between real experiments gained nothing from seeing their covariates.
  • A planted-effect check: the test detects a GC-shaped effect from 0.014 of a track's variance, so that null is real. For level it has no power below about 0.07.
What it means for CAC
Observed experiment
A per-experiment level curve plus a GC-dependent term recovers up to about 0.08 of the 0.17 that belongs to the experiment.
Experiment never done
This is the imputation task. Max found no way to predict the experiment's part: a typical value recovers nothing of the GC term and about a tenth of level error.
Sprint 10 · the ChIP-seq control

The model uses the ChIP-seq control to predict the experiment, not the cell.

  • The control helps only when it comes from the target experiment's own lab.
  • Training with the control hidden at random roughly halved the loss when the control is missing. Four runs could not tell whether it costs anything when the control is present.
  • Single-cell populations and most new datasets have no control, so the setting without the control is the realistic one.
Sprint 10 · counts to p-values · bears on our count-only model

The formula from counts to p-values is now exact. Predicted counts are not yet good enough.

  • Max's copy of the MACS2 formula (the ENCODE peak caller) had two details wrong. With them fixed it reproduces reprocessed tracks exactly from read positions.
  • On pooled targets the count route loses: the model predicts one replicate, and its counts draw peaks too weak or too strong relative to background.
  • On single-replicate targets a generic control (the mean of the training controls) works as well as the target's own.

Sprint 15 · the referee-style review · AlphaGenome 2

The control alone is worth about three times the gap to AlphaGenome 2.

  • Leak 1, the control. It is not recorded whether CANDI had the target's own control in that comparison. Headline numbers will give CANDI no control from the target's lab.
  • Leak 2, the sequence encoder. Borzoi trained on 132 of the 178 test tracks. The main figure will show the subset the encoder never saw.
  • The fix: one primary comparison written down before scoring, on tracks and chromosomes not yet scored, with a plain sentence for a loss.
Sprint 15 · what the three reviews changed

The reviews removed claims that could not lose.

  • Benchmark v3 is spent. It was used to design the model. A fresh train, validation and test split is needed, and test is touched once at the end.
  • Retrained rivals never drop. Each rival must first reproduce its published numbers.
  • Two challenge rows. One with only what entrants had (no pretrained encoder, no blind controls), one with the encoder. No blending with the training average in the headline row.
  • "Only CANDI predicts donor differences" was false. Classic imputers also predict per sample. The claim now needs baselines and a second donor set.
  • Model chosen on validation only. Acceptance tests are written and dated before any candidate is trained.
  • Fact check. About 160 statements traced; 2 wrong, 16 had lost a qualifier.
