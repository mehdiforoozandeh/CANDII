Phase 2 found one route that beat old D on pairs it never trained on: old D read through the base. Phase 3 asks the scale question on that route alone. One arm grows g, the small network that turns the processing settings into the CNN's scale-and-shift values. The other arm grows f, the CNN itself.

The deck follows the order of a paper: motivation, method, results (one slide per metric), discussion.

Motivationwhat phase 2 showed

CRPS is the error of the whole predicted distribution, in counts; lower is better. Dark is the oracle-scaled part (the shape), light is the scale error (a wrong total). Black ticks are the three seeds.

Never-trained pairs are arm to arm pairs that the model never saw in training. Old D direct maps the source arm straight to the target. Old D through the base chains two of old D's trained maps: arm to base, then base to target.

Phase 2 deferred the scale question: is the small g or the small CNN what limits this route? Phase 3 asks it.

Motivationthe two arms

Each arm grows one part only: g gets wider, or f gets more channels

g is a small fully connected network. It reads the settings of the source and the target and outputs the scale-and-shift values (FiLM values) that set the CNN. f is the CNN that maps the source track to the predicted distribution.

Bigger g: g's two hidden layers go from a width of sixty-four to two hundred and fifty-six; f is as in phase 2. Bigger f: f keeps its four layers, kernel and dilations, but doubles its channels. FiLM carries one scale and one shift per channel per layer, so g's output layer doubles too; g's hidden width stays as in phase 2. The receptive field does not change.

No combined arm and one size per axis (PI). A combined arm or a second size follows only if an axis helps.

Methodthe arms

The reference is phase 2's old D, not rerun. No phase-2 old D run came near its cap, so its cap and the new cap act the same unless a bigger model trains longer; metric 9 checks that.

Each arm runs on each per-track g and on the one g across tracks, with three seeds. Seeds do not pair across arms: a bigger model draws more initial weights, so seed zero of one arm is not seed zero of another. The learning rate is unchanged from phase 2; that is a caveat, not a tuned choice.

Methodthe route, the pairs, and the scoring

Training sees only base ↔ arm pairs; a never-trained arm → arm pair is predicted directly, or through the base

Panel (a): the direct route uses one map, from the source's settings to the target's. The route through the base uses two of the same model's trained maps: source to base, then the predicted base to the target. It needs no training of its own.

Panel (b): each track was rebuilt many times, changing one processing setting (a knob) at a time; each rebuild is an arm. Training uses pairs between the base and one arm, both ways. The depth arm at 7.5M reads is held out. Arm to arm pairs are never trained.

Panel (c): each run keeps the step with the best validation likelihood on chromosome 22. Every score is on chromosomes 19 and 21, with the blacklist removed.

Methodhow to read every number

Every CRPS carries its split, every gap a noise label, and a gain must clear a bar set before the runs

CRPS (continuous ranked probability score) compares the whole predicted distribution of each bin with the observed count. Lower is better. Pooled CRPS: per seed, the mean over a track's pairs, then over tracks; the reading is the mean over seeds. Oracle-scaled part: the CRPS left after the one best rescaling of the prediction (the shape). Scale error: what that rescaling removes (a wrong total).

Noise rule: the seed spread of a cell is the largest gap between its three seeds. A gap counts only if it is larger than two times the larger seed spread of the two cells.

Pre-registered reading (design check, before any run): for one g per track, a gain counts above two times the larger of the reference's seed spread and the arm's own, and never below a floor of about three percent. For one g across tracks, the reference's own seed spread is large, so the bar is high; that cell is reported but read per knob. "Within noise" means "no gain above the stated threshold", never "no effect".

Results(c) metric 2 · the generalisation gap

What it measuresNever-trained CRPS minus trained CRPS, same model, direct. The never-trained side uses same-knob pairs.
Best caseNear 0, and no worse than old D: unseen pairs are as good as trained ones.
Worst caseLarge and positive: the fit stays on the trained pairs.

Bars: the pooled gap, with seed ticks. Below zero means the never-trained pairs score better than the trained ones. The routes through the base have no trained pairs of their own, so this metric is read on the direct runs.

Results(f) metric 5 · the depth law (a sanity check)

What it measures|log2 predicted scale − log2 true read ratio| on depth pairs. Half the reads should halve every bin.
Best caseNear 0 in every pair group: the model scales counts by the read ratio.
Worst caseNear 1 or more: off by a factor of 2, as a map that returns its input would be.

Each row has up to four thin bars, one per pair group: trained, the held-out 7.5M, never-trained depth to depth, and never-trained pairs that touch 7.5M. Ticks are the seeds. Log scale. The routes through the base exist only for never-trained pairs.

Results(g) metric 9 · was the step cap the limit?

What it measuresThe share of runs whose best step is in the last 10 % of the cap, and the median best step against the reference's.
Best case0: every run found its best step well before the cap, so the reference's lower cap does not confound the comparison.
Worst caseRuns whose best step is above the reference's cap: those are step-confounded and listed apart.

Each dot is one run's best step (by its own validation likelihood on chromosome 22); each small tick is where that run stopped. Early stopping (an evaluation every 250 steps, patience 4) ends a run after four evaluations with no gain. The lines mark the reference's cap, which is also the flag for a step-confounded run, the start of the last 10 %, and the new cap.

Discussionwhat each metric tells us

One line per metric, restated from the results. Tier 1 (metric 1) is the question. Tier 2 (metrics 2 to 5) checks that nothing else got worse. Tier 3 (metric 9) checks that the steps do not confound the comparison.

option B

option C
Tune the learning rate for the bigger models
The spec left it unchanged as a caveat. A short sweep per arm would teach whether size is held back by the recipe, at the cost of a new grid.

The optimisation-failure reading is the design check's rule: an arm that does not improve through the base and also gets worse on trained pairs reads as an optimisation failure, not as "capacity does not help".

The options carry no recommendation and no ranking; the PI chooses.
