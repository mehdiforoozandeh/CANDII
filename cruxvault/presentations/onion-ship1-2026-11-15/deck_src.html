Methods(a) data: the frozen dataset

The dataset holds the tracks of the ENCODE Imputation Challenge, rebuilt from the reads of each published experiment. Panel a counts tracks per role against the challenge's list. Panel b counts tracks per assay class, and how many assays of each class can be scored. An assay is imputation-scorable when it has enough validation tracks with two read halves and enough training tracks. Every absent track was still running at the freeze; none failed a check. Absent tracks go into later dataset versions, and no result of this run uses them.

Methods(b) data: built from raw reads

Every track was rebuilt from its reads with the standard ENCODE pipeline of its assay, then split into processing views and read halves

    A view is one way to process the reads of one experiment: all reads, single-end only, reads cut shorter, fewer reads, no duplicate removal, or no mapping-quality filter. Each view is split at random into two disjoint read halves, called pseudoreplicates. A correction pair maps one view to another view of the same experiment. Experiments whose reads the portal does not serve started from ENCODE's alignments without read sequence, so they have the alignment-level views only.

    Methods(c) evaluation: regions, items, pairs, two tiers

    Panel a: each tick is one evaluation region, drawn to scale on its chromosome; orange ticks are the screening subset that runs the pair tests. Grey ticks are the fit regions, where the baselines fit their parameters; no scored bin lies in them. Panel b: an imputation item hides one validation track and gives the model the same cell's training tracks of other assays, or only its accessibility track. A correction pair asks for another processing view of the same experiment; its source is read from one read half and its target from the other half, so they share no read. A replicate pair measures the variance between two replicates.

    Models train on training tracks; every score uses validation tracks; the blind tracks are read once, at the end.

    Methods(d) evaluation: three goals, seven headline metrics

    Each goal has a lead metric on a scale from zero to one; the composite weighs imputation twice as much as each other goal

    Imputation

    Recovered share of specific variance above the noise floor: the part of the cell-type-specific variance a prediction explains, after the read-sampling noise of the truth (the noise floor) is removed. Zero = no specific signal; one = all that is recoverable.

    Specificity correlation: correlation of truth and prediction after both lose the training average at each position.

    Count CRPS skill against the position average. CRPS (continuous ranked probability score) is the error of a whole predictive distribution; it is always split into its oracle-scaled part and its scale-error part (the part one constant rescale removes).

    Covariate correction

    Gap closed on never-trained pairs: where the prediction's correction CRPS sits between depth-only scaling (zero) and the half-against-half floor (one). A never-trained pair is a combination of views that training never sees.

    Depth-law error: how far the predicted total count is from the true depth ratio, on a log scale.

    Calibrated uncertainty

    PIT deviation, pooled and in the worst region stratum. PIT (probability integral transform) is the predicted cumulative probability at the observed count; for a calibrated prediction it is uniform.

    Predicted over replicate variance: at least one against read halves, at most one against a second experiment.

    Each metric is a macro average over the scorable assays, with a cluster-bootstrap confidence interval over cells, that is, cells are resampled with replacement. Each model metric will also carry its range across seeds, that is, the maximum minus the minimum over seeds. An arm beats the incumbent only when its composite is higher by more than twice that range and no goal drops by more than twice its own range.

    Results(a) the variance layers on real data, per assay

    • Caveats. The values are on log1p counts at the depth of one read half, so at full depth the sampling layer is smaller. The replicate layer pools sequencing, library and biological replicate. The cell-type layer also holds donor and lab, because pairs that separate them are absent.

    A variance layer is the part of a track's variance that one source adds. Each layer is the difference of two pair values: the sampling layer comes from two read halves of one library; the replicate layer is isogenic replicates minus that; the cell-type layer is pairs of different cell types minus the replicates. Panel a shows each layer's share of the three; panel b shows the increments themselves with their cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals over cells. The ChIP-seq control has no cell-type layer, as expected.

    Results(b) the cell-type layer by region class and by scale

    The cell-type layer is small per bin but leads at the broad scale, and it is largest in promoters for accessibility and the active marks

    • Meaning for the goals. A score that pools all scales at the finest bin is dominated by sampling noise; the signal an imputer must explain sits at the broad scale and in regulatory regions.

    Each cell is the cell-type share of the three layers for one assay. Panel a splits the genome into region classes at the finest scale. Panel b splits the signal into scale bands, from single bins to domains of many kilobases; the bands add up exactly to the whole. A share can leave the range from zero to one when one increment in a band is negative. The telescoping check, that is, whether the layers add up to the total, left no group unexplained.

    Results(c) the training-free baselines

    Four baselines need no training, and the scorer gives the two reference baselines exactly zero and one

      A baseline is a predictor with no learned parameters beyond one dispersion per assay or per pair. The marginal negative binomial predicts the same distribution at every position, so it carries no position information. The position average is the mean of the training cells at each position, scaled to the target's depth. Depth-only scaling and the half-against-half floor apply to correction pairs only; they define the zero and the one of the correction scale. A negative binomial is a count distribution with a mean and an extra-dispersion parameter.

      The checks are identities the scorer must meet by construction. A second method, a separate computation from the per-pair records, reproduced the scorer's macro values exactly.

      Results(d) what the baselines reach on imputation and uncertainty

        Panel a: sampling noise as a share of the cell-type-specific variance of the truth, per scorable assay. This share bounds what any predictor can recover. Panel b: the count CRPS of the two imputing baselines, split into the oracle-scaled part and the scale-error part. Panel c: the pooled and worst-stratum PIT deviation, lower is better. The specificity correlation of the position average is zero by construction, because the position average is the quantity it removes. Signal-space scores are undefined for every baseline, because no baseline predicts a fragment length.

        Results(e) correction: the room per covariate knob

        Depth-only scaling falls behind the floor when the target is deeper than the source, and leads it when the target is shallower

          A knob is the set of processing covariates that differ between the source and the target of a pair: read length, mapping-quality filter, duplicate removal, single-end reads, and read depth. The gap is the CRPS of depth-only scaling minus the CRPS of the floor, as a mean over the pairs of the knob, with a cluster-bootstrap confidence interval over cells. Scaling a shallow source up multiplies its sampling noise by the depth ratio; that is why the sign follows the depth direction. The source gives no oracle-scaled and scale-error split per knob; the macro split is on the next slides. The expectation written before the run, that the depth and run-type knobs have the largest gap and subset pairs a gap near zero, is not supported as written.

          Results(f) calibration of the baselines

          The position average is closer to calibration than the marginal negative binomial, and both predict at least the variance between read halves

            Panels a and b: one curve per scorable assay. The curve is the average non-randomized PIT distribution function; a calibrated predictor lies on the diagonal. A curve below the diagonal at the middle means the observed count lies above the predicted median too often. Panel c: the predicted variance over the variance between two read halves; a calibrated predictor has a ratio of at least one. The upper bound needs a second experiment of the same cell, which this dataset lacks.

            Results(g) the room left for a model, per goal

            The position average is the reference a model must beat on all three goals

            Each card gives the best training-free value with its confidence interval, the bound that the data set, and what that leaves. These are the reference terms that every model score of shipment two is compared with.

            Open pointthe scale of correction gap closed

              The floor predicts the second read half from the first read half, so its mean carries one half's sampling noise. The position average is a smooth mean over many cells, so its mean carries almost none. On these pairs the floor is therefore not a lower bound on the correction CRPS. Panel b shows the scorer's own computation: a ratio of means per assay, then a mean over the assays. An assay whose denominator is small gives a large ratio. You are deciding how to rescale the metric; this slide proposes nothing.

              Results(h) AlphaGenome 2: the training-track audit

              AlphaGenome 2 trained on most of our validation tracks; the fair comparison uses only the tracks it never saw, kept apart from the rest

                AlphaGenome 2 takes DNA and a named condition and predicts every track of that condition; observed tracks of the cell are not an input. The audit asks, per track, whether its released model listed a training track of the same condition and mark. The audit works at the level of condition and channel, because no accession list is public, and the API serves the model that trained on all observed tracks. Panel c keeps the fair set and the trained-on set apart; only the fair set can carry a claim about unseen tracks.

                Results(i) region selection, interim

                At this window size, sampling training windows by peak classes is almost uniform sampling

                  A sampling list gives each genome tile a probability of being drawn as a training window, with a loss weight that keeps the objective unbiased. The effective sample size per draw is one for uniform sampling and falls as the list tilts away from uniform. This is a property of the data, measured before any training run; it says nothing about model accuracy. The continuous tilts replaced the class lists in the selection arms, which are trained and wait for their scores.

                  Results(j) screening set against full set, baselines only

                    The screening set runs the pair tests on one region in sixteen and keeps the imputation items on all regions. For each baseline the panel shows the value on the full set, its confidence interval, and the value on the screening set. This look uses predictors that do not learn, so it cannot say whether the screening set ranks models the same way. The check that decides that, written before any model was scored, compares model runs and has not run.
