peeling the onion · lab meeting

Where does the variance of an epigenomic signal come from? One identity, the law of total variance, splits it into layers.

This deck is about the formulation and its checks. Every number on the slides is either a design constant or comes from a simulation, and it is marked as such. No model has been trained yet, and the real-data layers are not collected yet.

The plan is to build the idea from a two-level example, then extend it to the full chain of nested factors, then show how replicate pairs measure each term, and what that means for a model's predicted variance.

Motivationthe question

A count in one bin varies for nested reasons, from biology down to read sampling

  • Imputation must predict the biological layers from other assays of the cell.
  • Covariate correction must remove what the lab and the processing add. Covariates are recorded settings such as depth and read length.
  • Calibrated uncertainty must match the variance that the inputs cannot explain.

To score each goal fairly, we need the size of each layer, measured on the same scale.

The onion picture is the PI's framing: each ring is a source of variation, and the evaluation should peel the total variance into these rings. The three goals of the model map onto different rings.

Methodthe law of total variance in two levelssimulated

Total variance = variance of the group means + mean variance inside a group

Simulated: three cell types, tracks each, one bin, Poisson counts. The identity holds exactly for any data with a finite variance (difference here: ).

Panel (a) shows the counts of one bin across tracks, grouped by cell type. The spread of the group means is the between-group term. The spread of the dots around their own group mean is the within-group term. Panel (b) shows that the two terms add up to the total. Inside a cell type the only source here is Poisson read sampling, so the within-group variance is close to the group mean.

Methodseven nested factorssimulated bar

Applied once per step of a chain of nested factors, the law splits the variance into eight terms

Each set in the chain contains the one before it. The first term is the variance of the across-cell mean, which depends only on position and assay. Each middle term is the variance that one more factor adds on average. The last term is read sampling, which is Poisson given the rate.

The bar is the simulator of the audit, with its planted layer sizes. It is an illustration, not a measurement.

Methodthe sampling term and depthsimulated

Read sampling grows with depth, every other layer with depth squared, so pairs are compared at one depth

  • Given every factor of the chain, reads are independent draws, so a bin's count is Poisson (variance equals mean): Ts = E[Y].
  • Any variance beyond Poisson belongs to a layer, for example PCR duplicates in the library layer. It never belongs to Ts.
  • The deeper track is binomially thinned (each read kept at random) to the shallower depth.

Under a multiplicative rate model, doubling the depth doubles the sampling term and quadruples every layer term. A pair value is therefore defined only at one stated depth, and two rungs are compared only at one depth. Pseudoreplicate halves sit at half the depth of the pooled track.

Methodthe pair valuesimulated chart

Half the mean squared difference of a pair measures every term below the level the pair shares

Two measurements that share a set of factors, and are otherwise independent draws of the same law, differ only by what lies below that set. The squared difference then averages to twice the conditional variance, so half of it is the expected conditional variance: the sum of the remaining terms.

The bars are the closed-form pair values of the simulator. The dots are the pair values computed directly from the simulated pairs. The halves rung sits at half depth, which is why it is shorter than the sampling term of the other bars.

Methoda layer is a difference

A layer is the difference of two consecutive pair values; donor and experiment are siblings, not a chain

In ENCODE, donor and experiment are crossed factors (each combines with each), not nested: one experiment can hold several donors, and one donor can appear in several experiments. So each of the two is measured over the isogenic rung, and the cell-type layer over a pair that shares only the cell type, built as anisogenic + other experiment − isogenic. That construction is exact when donor and experiment add without interaction.

Up to the isogenic rung, the ladder is a chain, and each layer is the step from the rung below. Above it, a nested order would force us to put donor before experiment or the reverse, and with crossed factors either order is wrong. A crossed linear mixed model, which needs no order, serves as the cross-check.

Methodthe graphical model

Biology and lab work set the rate of a bin; covariates and read sampling turn the rate into a count

An arrow means the child's effect on the bin's rate is drawn given its parents. The dotted link marks the crossed donor and experiment. The model sees its inputs S (for imputation, the other tracks of the cell and their covariates) and the target's covariates C′, and predicts the target's count.

The covariates sit outside the chain. Depth acts on read sampling. Read length, run type and pipeline settings act on which reads are counted. The variance that a change of covariates causes is a separate covariate layer, which the covariate-correction metric measures.

Methodwhat the predicted variance should equal

A calibrated predicted variance lies between the sampling term and the pair value of what the model knows

The negative binomial (NB) predicted variance is μ + μ2/n: the μ part is the Poisson sampling term, and the second part stands for the layers that the inputs do not determine. A model passes when the calibration ratio is at least one at the halves and at most one at the upper-bound rung.

Conditioning on more information never raises the expected conditional variance. So the variance that the model cannot remove is at least read sampling, and at most the pair value of the deepest level its inputs determine. The calibration ratio is the mean predicted variance over a rung's pair value, both at one depth and over the same bins. Neither bound alone means calibrated.

The model predicts each member of a pair from that member's own inputs and covariates. Subtracting the lower rung removes read sampling and the lower layers from both the numerator and the denominator. It is reported beside the headline metrics and does not enter the selection rule.

This uses a covariance, not a squared prediction difference, because a model that does not see the members' own reads cannot covary with their read sampling. It is computed on imputed targets only. Two biological replicates of one experiment get the same inputs from an imputation model, so their prediction difference is zero, and so is the captured part.

The calibration ratio thins the prediction to each pair's depth, then divides the mean predicted variance by the pair value. The error budget keeps its exact identities: truth variance = explained + unexplained + noise. Caveat: the identity is exact on counts; on log1p it holds only at one fixed depth, and its sampling term is no longer E[Y].

The bootstrap resamples cells, because pairs within a cell are not independent. A layer row records the depth at which it was taken, so it is never set against a metric taken at another depth. The scale bands are an orthonormal Haar split, which keeps the variance additive across bands.

Discussionassumptions and where they break

Each estimate is unbiased only under six assumptions; crossed factors and the log scale break the simple story

Exchangeable pair

Members are independent and identically distributed given what they share. Breaks for a depth subset of a parent track, which shares reads.

Equal depth

Both members, both rungs, model and truth at one depth. The tools thin the deeper track.

Order of conditioning

Donor and experiment are crossed in ENCODE; hence sibling layers, and a cell-type layer exact only without interaction.

Scale

Exact on counts. On log1p it holds only at one depth, and the sampling term then depends on the bin's rate.

Same cells and positions

A difference over two cell sets mixes a layer with the variance between cell sets.

Factor one half

Half the mean squared difference equals the conditional variance only for an exchangeable pair at equal depth.

These are the limitations of the formulation. The two marked cards are where the simple onion picture fails: the factors are not a single chain, and the scorer works on a log scale where the identity is no longer additive across depths.
