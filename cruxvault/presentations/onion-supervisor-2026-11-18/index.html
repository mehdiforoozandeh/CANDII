2 · objectivesone objective, three scores

The three goals became one training objective and three scores

  • Did: one Negative Binomial likelihood on three kinds of target: a hidden track (imputation), another view of a track (artifact correction), hidden bins of the same view (denoising).
  • Did: one score per goal: variance recovered above the read-sampling floor; gap closed on view pairs never seen in training; calibration of the predicted distribution.
  • Know now: the three goals train together in one small model, and no other loss beats the plain likelihood on two or more models.

Correction means: given covariates C of the input and C-prime of the wanted output, predict the same track as if it was measured under C-prime. Uncertainty is aleatoric: the model predicts a distribution per bin, not a point value.

3 · eval(a) the metric set

A metric set with baselines and a selection rule fixed before any run

  • Did: one lead term per goal on a scale from a simple reference (0) to an ideal (1), a composite, training-free baselines, and a rule: adopt an arm only beyond twice the seed range.
  • Know now: the base models beat the position average on every term (composite 0.53 against 0.20).
  • Know now: 16 regions are not enough for the correction terms: the pre-written check failed, so correction is scored on all 256 regions. Signs agree in 48 of 50 cells.

The composite is 0.5 imputation, 0.25 correction, 0.25 calibration. A second correction score, the correction-only term, measures how much of the covariate cost the model closes against its own same-view score. The baselines are the position average, a marginal Negative Binomial, depth-only scaling and the read-half floor.

3 · eval(b) peeling the onion: the variance of each layer

The onion, measured: read sampling is the thick outer layer; cell type is the core

  • At 25 bp: of the variance between two cell types, read sampling is 93% (broad marks) and 86% (narrow marks); the biological-replicate layer is 1.2% and 1.8%; the cell-type layer is 6% and 12%.
  • The core grows with the scale: the cell-type layer is 3 to 4% at fine scale and 77% (broad) and 84% (narrow) at the broad scale.
  • Know now: most of what a 25 bp count shows is sampling noise; the biology that we want lives at larger scales. A second experiment from another lab and donor adds nothing that these pairs can resolve beyond a replicate.

How it is measured: a ladder of pairs. Two read halves of one track differ by read sampling only. Two biological replicates add the replicate layer. Two cell types add the cell-type layer. Each layer is the extra variance over the layer below, with both sides of a pair corrected. Shares are normalized to add to 100 percent. The cell-type layer is 0.00273, interval 0.0015 to 0.0040, for broad marks and 0.00355, interval 0.0020 to 0.0051, for narrow marks. Limits: the library and lane layers are not built yet; DNase-seq is not interpretable yet because one track with 15,201 reads sets the common depth; at the peak scale the broad-mark value is about zero.

3 · eval(c) the resolution question

Almost all variance below the 25 bp bin is read-sampling noise

  • Did: recounted 412 views at 1 bp and split the variance of each track by bin size (1, 5, 25, 50, 125 bp).
  • Know now: below 25 bp lies 0.93 (broad) and 0.90 (narrow) of the base-by-base variance; read sampling alone gives 0.96. Replicates reproduce about 0.1% of it.
  • Know now: from 25 to 125 bp, 7% (broad) and 13% (narrow) of the step is reproducible: a coarser bin starts to lose signal.

This answers the interesting question of the plan on the data side: a finer bin than 25 bp adds noise, not signal, for histone marks. The model-side test, 75 training runs, is ready for the next session. DNase-seq has one track and no replicate pair here, so accessibility is not answered.

4 · data(a) the 4th dimension: measurement

The EIC data, rebuilt with the measurement dimension

  • Did: every EIC experiment rebuilt from its reads with the standard ENCODE pipeline, checked by accession: 2,740 measurements (2,328 training, 334 validation, plus blind rows).
  • Did: designed views of the same reads, each with two read halves; 11 covariates per measurement; 15,123 pairs; 18 view combinations withheld from training to score correction.
  • Not done: the blind tracks (722 rows), 4 DNase-seq rows, and the extended dataset beyond EIC.

A measurement is one cell type, one assay, one way of processing. The 11 covariates are depth, run type, read length, duplicate removal, mapping-quality threshold, pipeline, pipeline version, lab, platform, antibody and donor. Add-ons: 71 second experiments, QC side tables, a 1 bp region store.

4 · data(b) do the counterfactual views help?

The views and the covariates are what make artifact correction work

  • Know now: training on the base view only halves the correction-only term: 0.650 to 0.309 (conv autoencoder), 0.616 to 0.301 (conv + attention); the U-Net shows the same.
  • Know now: scrambling the covariates in training cuts the same term on all three models, by 0.20 to 0.24.
  • Know now: imputation, calibration and the composite do not move.

All of this is on all 256 evaluation regions with 3 seeds, and the falls are more than twice the seed range on the two conv models. This is the direct evidence for the fourth dimension: the model uses the covariates, and the designed views teach it the correction.

5 · miniCANDI(a) the architecture

The prototype: counts, DNA and covariates in; a Negative Binomial out

  • Inputs: X, counts of all assays of one cell type in 1,024 bins of 25 bp; S, the DNA sequence through our own DNA tower; C and C-prime, covariates of the inputs and of the wanted outputs (injected by FiLM).
  • Output: two Negative Binomial parameters per bin and assay. Three prototypes of about 1 million parameters: conv autoencoder, conv + attention, U-Net.
  • Know now: no architecture wins on the composite (0.53, 0.53, 0.49). The U-Net imputes best (0.432 against 0.382 and 0.387); the conv models correct best (0.714 and 0.685 against 0.504).

This follows the specification in the plan. Hidden tracks are replaced by a learned token. The mean of the output carries the depth of C-prime. Each model was trained with 3 seeds on 80,000 windows. The choice of architecture for scaling is the first decision of the next session.

5 · miniCANDI(b) the ablations

16 ablations on three models: few switches matter, the simple recipe holds

  • Did: 39 single-switch arms, 3 seeds each: ChIP control in and out, average baseline input, two-step routing, fixed input depth, DNA source and fusion, covariate embedding and injection, losses, training views, count transforms.
  • Know now: most switches stay inside the seed range. The default count transform stays. Our own DNA tower is as good as frozen Nucleotide Transformer embeddings.
  • Know now: signal p-value and peaks can be derived from the predicted Negative Binomial; the formula is built and reviewed, the scoring of all runs is next.

The figure shows arm minus base in units of the seed range: blue is up, red is down. A number appears only where the difference is beyond the range.

5 · miniCANDI(c) settled on all regions

Four design rules are decided on all evaluation regions; two wait for scores

  • Settled, strong: the covariates are used for correction; all counterfactual views stay in training.
  • Decided: imputation weight 4 is a candidate for conv + attention only. Fixed input depth is a trade-off on the U-Net (correction +0.166, imputation -0.028); it enters no recipe yet.
  • Not decided: two-step routing and the average baseline input; 37 of 62 scorings are done, the rest run now.

Each rule was written before its scores existed. The design table has 31 choices, each with its evidence, its confidence, and the run that would settle it.

beyond the plan(b) where the views help, by pair type

Views help where they match the pair type

  • Depth-thinning views make the U-Net reliable on depth pairs: every seed trained with them scores well there, every seed without them does not.
  • Alignment-level views alone lower imputation on both conv models (0.340 and 0.323 against 0.382 and 0.387) and break the U-Net on their own pairs.
  • Two-step routing through the base view, the idea from the earlier covariate-correction testbed, lowers correction on both conv models in screening (-0.115, -0.059) at twice the compute.

This is the finer answer to the question whether the counterfactual views help. A view teaches the correction of its own kind of difference. Two-step routing is not decided on all regions yet; its scorings are still running.

beyond the plan(c) inside the architectures

The U-Net trade-off comes from its skip connections

  • U-Net with skips that carry DNA only (all regions): correction rises from 0.504 to 0.715, imputation falls from 0.432 to 0.380. So the count skips buy imputation and cost correction.
  • Tuned recipes do not beat the base recipes: three levels in the conv autoencoder, attention at 128 positions, a higher learning rate in the U-Net all stay inside the seed range.
  • Training length: no model had converged at 80,000 windows; twice the windows lowered the held-out loss but not the composite.

This explains the tie on slide 9. The U-Net copies local count detail through its skips, which helps a hidden track and hurts when the output must differ from the input view. The loss figure shows that no loss arm changes this picture.

beyond the plan(d) other findings

Other findings that the plan did not ask for

  • AlphaGenome 2 as an outside competitor on tracks never trained on: specific correlation 0.438, against 0.222 for the position average and 0.333 for two read halves of the same track.
  • A bias in read halves at 1 bp: after duplicate removal the two halves of a track exclude each other at a single base, so they anti-correlate. Replicate pairs do not have this bias.
  • Signal p-value from counts: the first formula overstated the counting noise of a pileup; a corrected "box spread" formula is built and reviewed.

More, if asked. One DNase-seq track with only 15,201 reads sets the common depth of every DNase-seq pair, so those layer values wait for a rerun. The QC side tables fill strand cross-correlation for 89 percent of the rows and library complexity for 38 percent. Scoring on the second cluster was ten times slower, because its jobs wait on input and output.
