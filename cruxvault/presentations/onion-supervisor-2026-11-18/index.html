What we know now

  • Read sampling is by far the largest layer at 25 bp. The replicate layer and the cell-type layer are small beside it (slides 5 and 6).
  • Lab and donor change together in ENCODE, so the pure lab effect has too few pairs. We report lab with donor as one effect.
  • Per-library and per-lane units (sequencing and library layers) are not built yet: they are in the 722 rows of the next build.

2 · objectives

Three goals became one training objective and three scores

What we did

  • Imputation: predict a hidden track from the other tracks of the cell. Score: share of the cell-type-specific variance recovered above the read-sampling floor.
  • Artifact correction: given covariates C of the input and C' of the wanted output, predict the same track as if measured under C'. Score: gap closed between plain depth scaling and the read-sampling limit, on view pairs never seen in training.
  • Calibrated uncertainty: the model outputs a Negative Binomial per bin. Score: how close its probability integral transform is to uniform.
  • One loss for all three: Negative Binomial likelihood on hidden tracks, on other views, and on hidden bins of the same view.

What we know now

  • The three goals can be trained together in one small model and scored separately.
  • No other loss beats the plain Negative Binomial likelihood on two or more models.

3 · eval (a)

A metric set with baselines and a fixed selection rule

What we did

  • One lead term per goal, each on a scale from a simple reference (0) to an ideal (1), plus a composite (0.5 imputation, 0.25 correction, 0.25 calibration).
  • A second correction score, the correction-only term: how much of the covariate cost the model closes against its own same-view score.
  • Training-free baselines scored on the same set: position average, marginal Negative Binomial, depth-only scaling, the read-half floor.
  • A selection rule fixed before any run: adopt an arm only if the composite rises by more than twice the seed range and no goal falls by more than twice its range.
  • 256 evaluation regions; a pre-written check of whether 16 regions are enough.

What we know now

  • The base models beat the position average on every term: composite 0.53 against 0.20.
  • 16 regions are not enough for the correction terms: the check failed its rule. Correction is now scored on all 256 regions.
  • On the decided arms, 16 and 256 regions agree in sign in 48 of 50 cells.

3 · eval (b) · variance decomposition

Peeling the layers: read sampling dominates; replicate and cell-type layers are small

What we did

  • A ladder of pair values: two read halves, two biological replicates, two cell types. Each rung is the extra variance over the rung below.
  • Assay-matched pairs, 95% bootstrap intervals over cell types, no pooling across assay classes.

What we know now

  • Earlier pass at 25 bp: read sampling was 76% to 97% of the variance per assay; the replicate layer 0.1% to 6%.
  • Broad histone marks: the sampling rung is 0.0463; the replicate rung adds 0.0005 (76 pairs).
  • Cell-type rung: 0.00273 [0.0015, 0.0040] for broad marks (355 pairs), 0.00355 [0.0020, 0.0051] for narrow marks (445 pairs).
  • A second experiment from another lab and donor adds no variance that these pairs can resolve beyond a biological replicate.
  • Limit: DNase-seq rows are not interpretable yet (one track with 15,201 reads sets the common depth).

3 · eval (c) · the resolution question

Almost all variance below the 25 bp bin is read-sampling noise

What we did

  • Recounted 412 views at 1 bp and split the variance of each track by bin size (1, 5, 25, 50, 125 bp).
  • Used biological replicates to separate reproducible signal from noise.

What we know now

  • Below 25 bp lies 0.93 (broad marks) and 0.90 (narrow marks) of the base-by-base variance. Read sampling alone gives 0.96.
  • Replicates reproduce only about 0.1% of that variance. A finer bin adds noise, not signal, for histone marks.
  • From 25 to 125 bp, 7% (broad) and 13% (narrow) of the step is reproducible: a coarser bin starts to lose signal.
  • So 25 bp is a sound choice on the data side. The model-side test (75 runs) is ready for the next session.

4 · data (a) · the 4th dimension

The EIC data, rebuilt with the measurement dimension

What we did

  • Rebuilt every EIC experiment from its reads with the standard ENCODE pipeline of its assay, checked by accession.
  • 2,740 measurements: 2,328 training, 334 validation, plus the blind rows. A measurement is one cell type, one assay, one way of processing.
  • Designed views of the same reads (single-end, crop 36 and 50, depth 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, no duplicate removal, no mapping-quality filter), each with two read halves.
  • 11 covariates per measurement: depth, run type, read length, duplicate removal, mapping-quality threshold, pipeline, pipeline version, lab, platform, antibody, donor.
  • 15,123 pairs; 18 view combinations are withheld from training and used only to score correction.
  • Add-ons: 71 second experiments; QC side tables; a 1 bp region store.

Not done yet

  • The blind tracks (722 rows) and 4 DNase-seq rows; the extended dataset beyond EIC.

4 · data (b)

Do the counterfactual views help? Yes, for artifact correction

What we know now (all 256 regions, 3 seeds)

  • Training on the base view only cuts the correction-only term by about half: 0.650 to 0.309 on the conv autoencoder, 0.616 to 0.301 on conv + attention. Both are more than twice the seed range; the U-Net shows the same.
  • Imputation, calibration and the composite do not move.
  • Scrambling the covariates in training also cuts the correction-only term on all three models (by 0.20 to 0.24). So the models do use the covariates.
  • This is the direct evidence for the 4th dimension: the views and the covariates are what make correction work.

5 · miniCANDI (a) · architecture

The prototype: counts, DNA and covariates in; a Negative Binomial out

Inputs and output (as in the plan)

  • X: counts of all assays of one cell type in a window of 1,024 bins of 25 bp (25.6 kb); some tracks hidden or missing.
  • S: the DNA sequence of the window, through our own small DNA tower.
  • C and C': the covariates of the input experiments and of the wanted output experiments.
  • Output: two Negative Binomial parameters per bin and assay; the mean carries the depth of C'.
  • Covariates enter by feature-wise scaling and shifting (FiLM).

Three prototypes of about 1 million parameters

  • Conv autoencoder, conv + attention, U-Net. Each trained with 3 seeds on 80,000 windows.

What we know now

  • No architecture wins: the three tie on the composite (0.53, 0.53, 0.49).
  • A real trade-off: the U-Net imputes best (0.432 against 0.382 and 0.387); the conv models correct best (0.714 and 0.685 against 0.504).

5 · miniCANDI (b) · ablations

16 ablations on three models: few switches matter

What we did

  • 39 single-switch arms on three models, 3 seeds each: ChIP control as input and output, average baseline input, two-step routing, fixed input depth, DNA on, off and fused in four ways, covariate embedding and injection, mask mode, losses, training views, nine count transforms.

What we know now

  • Most switches stay inside the seed range. The simple recipe holds.
  • The default count transform stays: two alternatives lower imputation on all three models.
  • Our own DNA tower is as good as frozen Nucleotide Transformer embeddings.
  • Signal p-value and peaks can be derived from the predicted Negative Binomial; the formula is built and reviewed, the scoring of all runs is next.
