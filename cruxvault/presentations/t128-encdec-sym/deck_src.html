A symmetric encoder-decoder: one g, one CNN, and a learned clean setting in the middle

Phase 1 of the redesign of the t126 encoder-decoder: 8 settings, both versions of g, . This is an exploratory pass: it reports readings, not verdicts.

Navigate with → ← or space  ·  n shows the speaker notes

This deck follows the encoder-decoder deck (t126). There, building composition into g did not carry the fit to pairs the model never trained on. The PI redesigned the encoder-decoder so that both halves are the same network, set by one g, and meet at a learned "clean" setting.

Every number on the slides and in these notes is read from the result files when the deck is built. Some runs were still training at build time; each chart shows its own run count.

Motivationtwo words first

The model trains only on base ↔ arm pairs; arm → arm pairs are never seen in training

Panel (a): each track was rebuilt many times, changing one processing setting (a knob) at a time; each rebuild is an arm. Training uses only pairs between the base track and one arm, in both directions (solid). A pair between two arms (dashed) is never trained: the model must work it out from the settings alone.

Panel (b): the depth law. With fewer reads, every bin scales by the read ratio. We compare the predicted total, over the source total, with the true read ratio on a log2 scale. 0 is perfect.

Card 2 is the PI's ruling: the encoder and decoder must be symmetric, and one g must set both.

Motivationthe redesign

The redesign: every track passes through one learned clean setting, with the same network on both sides

Left, t126: two g's, each reading one side, set two different halves of a cut CNN.

Right, t128: one g, the same g as in the t118 grid, reads a pair of settings as always. The trick is the middle: g is asked for the map from the source's settings c to a learned clean setting C_clean, and then for the map from C_clean to the target's settings c′. Both maps use the same CNN. So an arm → arm pair uses exactly the two pieces that the trained pairs use: c → C_clean and C_clean → c′.

Methodthe model

The encoder and the decoder are one CNN with shared weights; only the FiLM from g differs

f is old rung D exactly: 4 dilated convolution layers (dilations 1, 2, 4, 8), 32 channels, kernel 5, and a 1 × 1 head. FiLM means g gives a scale and a shift for every layer and channel; g gives nothing else.

Encoder: input x = log(1 + X); FiLM from g(c, C_clean); the head gives a Gaussian mean μ_z and spread σ_z per 25 bp bin. Decoder: input Z; FiLM from g(C_clean, c′); the head gives the negative binomial of X′. Z has 1 channel at the same 25 bp resolution, with no pooling.

Two f's in series see further, so each training window gets 60 bins of halo on each side.

Methodthe clean setting C_clean

C_clean is learned per track: form A starts at 0 and is free, form D starts at the base inside a hard box

C_clean is a covariate vector like any other. Its assay entries are copied from the source; only the 13 processing entries are learned. With one g per track, there is one C_clean per track; with one g across tracks, one per track as well.

Form A (the default): starts at 0, which in z units is the mean of the training settings, and moves freely. Form D: starts at the base track's own settings, and each entry is a sigmoid onto the range seen in training, so it cannot leave that box. There is no penalty weight.

Methodthe losses

Three losses: the cross map (L1), the round trip (L2), and latent agreement (L3); a VAE adds noise to Z

For one trained pair, the source (X, c) and the target (X′, c′): Z = encoder of X with c, Z′ = encoder of X′ with c′.

L1, cross: decode Z with c′ and score X′; decode Z′ with c and score X. That is the real job. L2, round trip: decode Z with c and score X itself, and the same for Z′. L3, latent agreement: λ times the mean squared gap between Z and Z′, so that the two tracks of one pair meet at one latent.

VAE: Z is sampled from the Gaussian, with a KL term to N(0, 1) whose weight ramps from 0 to 1 over the first 1000 of 4000 steps. The deterministic arms use Z = μ_z.

Methodthe settings

Eight settings change one thing at a time from the default; two of them have a no-covariates twin

settingclean settingcross (L1)round trip (L2)agreement (L3)latent Zruns doneof assigned

Setting 1 is the default; each other setting changes one or two things from it. Settings 3 and 4 test the round trip alone, which the spec flagged: with 1 channel and a skip that starts at the identity, the round trip alone may stay at the identity.

The no-covariates twin is the same model fed random settings: it can only learn one average map. Twins were trained only for settings 1 and 3.

Both versions of g: one g per track (trained on one track's arms), and one g across all tracks.

Methodwhat we score, and how a run is selected

We score trained pairs, a held-out depth, and never-trained pairs; each setting picks its step by its own loss on chr22

Panel (a): the depth ladder. Trained pairs link the base to two depths. The 7.5M depth is held out of training: its pairs with the base are only scored. Never-trained pairs link two depths, with or without the held-out one.

Panel (b): selection. Each setting keeps its best step by its own loss's likelihood terms on chromosome 22, with Z = μ_z, and without the KL or λ terms, so the λ values compare fairly (PI ruling). The same rule picks one λ per version of g.

Methodreferences and the noise rule

Two references need no training: old D, and old D chained through the base

Old D is t126's retrained old rung D, with 7.5M held out, and its twin. Old D through the base chains two of old D's trained maps, arm → base, then base → arm, on the never-trained pairs. It is the target the redesign aims at: composition through a middle, done by hand.

The noise rule (spec): a gap counts only if it is larger than 2 × the larger seed spread of the two cells, where a seed spread is the largest gap between the 3 seeds. Smaller gaps are shown and labelled "within noise".

Results(a) the depth law

Each row is one setting or reference; each bar is the median depth-law error over that pair group, with the 25th to 75th percentile as a whisker. The small ticks are each seed's own median: a gap smaller than their spread is not a gap. Log scale. The number after a name is its run count in this version of g.

Results(b) never-trained pairs, all knobs pooled

CRPS on counts over every never-trained pair: mean over a track's pairs, then over tracks, then over seeds. Lower is better. Dark is the oracle-scaled part (what is left after the best single rescale); light is the scale error (what that rescale removes). Ticks are the seeds. The dashed lines mark old D and old D through the base.

Results(b) never-trained pairs, per knob

Each small panel is one knob's never-trained pairs (arm → arm of the same knob), and the last is pairs across two knobs. Each panel has its own x-axis. Bars are split as on the last slide: dark oracle-scaled, light scale error. The control and caller knobs do not change the counts, so they are left out.

Results(c) gain over the no-covariates twin

Gain over the twin, per pair: (twin's CRPS − real CRPS) ÷ twin's CRPS, then the median over pairs. Above 0, the settings help; at 0, the model ignores them.

Filled dot: trained pairs. Open ring: never-trained pairs. A solid line joins them when the gap passes the noise rule; a dotted line means "within noise".

Results(d) collapse: is Z just x?

Z is the encoder's mean μ_z, computed on chromosomes 19 and 21 for every product of a run's tracks. x = log(1 + X) is the encoder's own input. Panel (a): the median gap between them, per bin. Panel (b): their correlation. If Z equals x up to a shift, the latent holds the input and nothing more: we call that collapse.

Results(e) latent agreement

For two products of the same track (two arms, or an arm and the base), we take the median gap between their latents, |Z − Z′|, and the median gap between their inputs, |x − x′|. If the encoder removes the settings, the latents of one sample should agree more than the inputs do: the dot lands left of the input line.

Results(f) the swap check against the identity distance

Swap check (y): give the model c′ = c, so nothing should change, and measure how far the prediction moves from X: the median over bins with X > 0 of |log(predicted mean ÷ X)|. 0 passes.

Identity distance (x): the same measure on real pairs, c ≠ c′, against the source X. A map that returns its input has 0 here. The target is the bottom right: pass the swap check while staying far from the identity. The diagonal is where a model changes X as much when nothing should change as when the settings change.

Results(g) the λ choice

The rule (spec and PI ruling): on chromosome 22, each run's best validation NLL of its L1 + L2 terms, with Z = μ_z and without the λ term; per version of g, the λ with the lowest mean over runs wins. All three λ are scored on every slide regardless.

Results(h) where C_clean went

Panel (a): each row is one processing entry of C_clean, in the z units g reads (0 = the mean of the training settings). The grey bar is the range seen in training, which is form D's box. The black diamond is the base track. Rings mark where each form started; dots, where it ended. Median over the tracks, one g per track.

Panel (b): per setting, the median distance C_clean moved from its start, against the median spread of the learned value over the 3 seeds.

Discussionwhat this rules in and out

The symmetric form passes the swap check far from the identity, but it does not yet beat old D on never-trained pairs

Ruled out
The round trip alone
Without the cross loss, the model stays at the identity: it ignores the settings, and the depth law misses by the full ratio. The VAE moves it only part of the way.
Ruled in
One shared CNN, set by one g, with the cross loss
It returns X when c′ = c, changes X when the settings change, and keeps its gain on never-trained pairs as a whole.
Not shown
A gain over old D, or a latent free of x
Never-trained CRPS sits within noise of old D, behind old D through the base; Z tracks x closely in every setting; C_clean barely moves.

These are readings from one exploratory pass, not verdicts. Each is open to the doubts on the next slide.

Ruled out: the spec's own doubt came true. With a skip that starts at the identity and only the round trip to fit, the identity is a perfect answer, and the model takes it.

Ruled in: the cross loss is what makes the shared network use the settings. The default passes the swap check while moving X on real pairs, the target the PI named.

Not shown: the never-trained pairs are no better than old D's, and chaining old D through the base by hand still does better. The latent stays close to x, and the clean setting stays near where it started.

Discussiondoubts

Five doubts could each change a reading

C_clean is not identifiable
Under form A, g can absorb any shift of C_clean, so its learned value may mean nothing. It also barely moved.
Under-training
Incomplete grid
Two clusters, three kits
Device and reruns
GPU type is not in the tables. Some runs were re-run after a timeout, a busy GPU node, or the form-D device bug; a rerun trains from scratch.

Kits: Nibi kit K1 at commit 58c9c22; Fir kit K2 at f47cf11; Fir kit K3 at 836e96c, which is K2 plus a one-line fix that put form D's box on the same device as its start. The code of each setting is the same in every kit it ran on.

Reruns (plan log): three default runs timed out at 4 h and were re-run with 8 h; the form-D array was re-submitted on K3 after the device bug; on Fir, runs on a busy or slow node were re-run with those nodes excluded. The device per run is recorded on the cluster, not in these tables.
