The deck runs in paper order: what we want, why the U-Net was stuck, how the model and its training work, what we found, and what is left. Every measured number is read from the score tables when the deck is built.

Motivationwhat we want from one model

One model must impute, correct and know its own uncertainty

Imputation predicts a track that is missing from the input. Correction maps an observed track, made with one protocol, to what a reference protocol would have measured. Calibration asks that the predicted Negative Binomial spread match the real errors. A recipe wins only if it is at least as good as the best base model on all three at once.

Methodone training example

Each window hides some tracks and asks for three kinds of target

A window is a stretch of genome from one cell type, with every assay of that cell as a track. A random fraction of tracks is hidden whole, and those are the imputation targets. The visible tracks are either corrected to another processing view of the same experiment, or reconstructed in the same view with a quarter of their bins hidden. The loss on a same-view target counts only the hidden bins, so the model cannot score by copying.

Methodthe architecture

A two-tower U-Net with covariates on both sides and a Negative Binomial head

Counts and DNA sequence go down separate towers and meet at the bottleneck, where a small transformer sees the whole window. The decoder goes back up to full resolution with skip connections from both towers, each scaled by the output covariates. A per-track head turns the features into a mean and a dispersion for each bin. The head skip, shown in red, brings each track's own input features straight to its head.

Methodthe per-track encoder

Each count becomes nine numbers before any bins or tracks mix

Each cell of the window, one bin of one track, is handled alone. The count is depth-normalized, lifted to eight features by a shared small network, tagged with which assay it is, and scaled by that experiment's covariates. Hidden bins and missing tracks are replaced by learned tokens before anything mixes, so no hidden value can leak. The first layer that mixes all tracks in a bin is the stem.

Methodthe objective

Read-disjoint pairs make copying the input useless

The loss is the Negative Binomial negative log-likelihood over the loss mask. Before this change, a correction pair was two processing views of the same reads, so the input already held most of the target's noise, and a model with a cheap identity path could win by copying. With read-disjoint pairs, the input is built from one random half of the reads and the target from the other half. Their noise is independent, so copying the input no longer predicts the target. Imputation pairs are unchanged.

Methodselection rule and arms

A recipe wins when it is above the floor on all three and clearly better on one

Pareto rule (fixed before the runs)
  • Floor per objective = best base model's mean minus its seed range (max minus min over seeds).
  • Eligible: at or above the floor on all three objectives.
  • Beyond on one objective: better than the best base by more than the larger of the two seed ranges.
  • PASS = eligible and beyond on at least one; NEAR = eligible, none beyond.
  • Correction screened on 16 genomic regions, confirmed on 256.

The rule was written down before any arm was scored. A seed range is the spread of a mean over the independent training restarts, so beyond means the gain is larger than the noise of either model. Screening uses few regions to be cheap; only arms that pass the screen are scored on the larger set.

    Results(d) where the U-Net copies

    The head skip carries the copy; pairs cut it, but not to the conv models' level

    Copy gain = (CRPS on hidden bins − CRPS on visible bins) / CRPS on hidden bins, same-view reconstruction; near 0 means no copying. CRPS: a proper score of a predictive distribution, lower is better.

    Panel a: the full U-Net has a copy gain far above the conv models. Removing the head skip alone brings it down to the conv models' level, so the head skip, not the level skips, carries the copy. Read-disjoint pairs cut it by most of the way, and the average-signal input cuts it further. Panel b is the earlier data version: when only DNA skips remain, correction rises and imputation falls. The open question is whether removing the head skip on top of the pairs keeps imputation; that run is training now.

    Results(e) what did not work

    These are kept because they rule things out. Moving FiLM conditioning around the network, adding learned covariate correction layers before or after the model, conditioning the skips and adding extra inputs did not stop the copy on their own. The one fix that just reached the floor is fixed input depth, which thins every input to one sequencing depth and was judged unrealistic for deployment. Head skip removal, which pays in imputation, and the training pairs moved correction well past the floor. The pairs also did not help the two conv models, whose correction was already high.

    Discussionwhat this rules in and out

    The trade-off came from the training pairs, not from the architecture

    Ruled in

    The U-Net's skips learned to copy because correction pairs shared reads. Read-disjoint pairs remove the reward for copying, and the U-Net keeps most of its imputation lead.

    Ruled out

    Fixing the trade-off by moving covariate conditioning, adding correction layers or conditioning the skips: none reached the correction floor. Only fixed input depth did, which needs every input thinned to one depth.

    Limitations

    One data version, three seeds per arm, one model size. The added inputs lower calibration and vary widely between seeds. The head skip still copies more than the conv models.

    Discussionwill scaling close the gap?

    Twice the training, before the pairs, made the U-Net copy more

    • U-Net correction moved from to with twice the windows, while imputation moved from to . Model size was never varied.

    This is the earlier data version with the older correction scale, and these runs predate the pairs. The held-out loss went down with more training, but none of the objectives improved, and the U-Net's correction got worse. Our reading is that more training let it learn the copy better. Whether scaling helps once copying is no longer rewarded is untested; that run is queued.

    Discussionoptions for the next phase

    Options for the next phase

    Option A · close the copy path

    Pairs with the head skip removed (training now), a blind-spot head skip (output at a bin never sees that bin's input), or skip dropout. Cheap; tells us whether the remaining copy costs correction.

    Option B · calibration of the added inputs

    Seed ensembling and post-training dispersion recalibration on held-out data. No training; tests whether the imputation gain of the added inputs can keep the pairs model's calibration.

    Option C · scale with pairs

    Two and four times the parameters, twice the windows, with read-disjoint pairs. About a thousand CPU-hours; tells us whether scaling helps once copying is not rewarded.

    These are options, not recommendations. Option A's first run and option C are already approved and queued.
