\title[Covariate artifact correction testbed]{Recorded processing covariates correct a track on the pairs they were trained on, but the correction does not carry to unseen pairs}

\author{\fnm{Mehdi} \sur{Foroozandeh}}
\affil{\orgname{CANDI project, private close-out draft, \today}}

\abstract{We asked whether recorded processing covariates carry enough information to map
one measurement of an epigenomic track onto another of the same material. We built
counterfactual arms of \lit{7} tracks, each changing one of \lit{10} processing settings, and
trained networks that read the covariates and output the correction. On trained depth pairs
the better designs close \gcBDLo{} to \gcBDHi{} of the gap from a covariate-free map to the
oracle (seed range at most \gcBDnoiseHi{} of the gap). On unseen pairs the gain over a scrambled-covariate twin falls from \relCntTr{} to
\relCntSame{}, and no design follows the depth law; three final experiments did not change this,
but a mappability input helps in low-mappability bins. All readings are exploratory, on
\lit{7} tracks.}

\keywords{epigenomics, experimental covariates, counterfactual data, sequencing depth}

\maketitle

\section{Motivation and background}\label{sec:motivation}

In the ENCODE Imputation Challenge, an average-activity baseline beat all but two of
\nEICmodels{} submitted models \cite{schreiber2023}. The cause was a small processing
difference: the single-end training data and the paired-end blind test data were
deduplicated by different rules. Processing settings (covariates) thus leave artifacts that a
model must correct.

CANDI imputes tracks from raw counts and reads covariates (depth, run type, read length) on
both sides. Inside CANDI this conditioning cannot be tested: its corpus pairs an experiment
only with thinned copies of itself, so only depth ever changes with the material fixed, and
the covariates are collinear (they change together). We therefore built an isolated testbed.
Our question: do the recorded covariates carry enough information to map one measurement of a
track onto another of the same material?

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Counterfactual arms.} We took \lit{7} challenge tracks (one DNase-seq, three narrow
and three broad histone marks), processed each to a base of \lit{30}M reads (DNase \lit{50}M)
and to arms that change one setting: depth (\lit{15}M, \lit{7.5}M, \lit{3.75}M), read length
(\lit{36}, \lit{50}~bp), mapping filter, duplicate removal, paired-end, an antibody proxy
(\lit{50}\% or \lit{90}\% control reads), fragment extension and three control settings:
\lit{130} products, each split into two random halves of its reads.

\paragraph{Model and competitors.} A generator $g$ reads source and target covariates $C$,
$C'$ and outputs a map $f$; $f(X)$ predicts the target as negative binomial (NB) counts per
\lit{25}~bp bin (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}a). Four designs of $f$ rise in capacity: per-bin affine
(A), per-bin monotone curve (B), kernel plus curve (C) and a conditioned convolutional network
(D). Competitors are a scrambled-covariate twin, one-warp (a covariate-free monotone map per
pair) and an oracle (exact binomial thinning for depth). The score is the NB CRPS (continuous
ranked probability score, distance from forecast to observation), always split into a shape
part and a scale error. A gain counts when it exceeds \lit{2}$\times$ the seed range (largest
minus smallest over \lit{3} seeds). Scoring uses held-out \lit{chr19} and \lit{chr21}, on
trained pairs and on unseen pairs (arm to arm, never paired in training). The depth law says a
depth change scales the predicted total by the depth ratio.

\paragraph{Final experiments.} Three claims were pre-registered (fixed in writing before any
run) on one model shared by all tracks (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}b): adding a cross-assay
imputation or masked-bin denoising objective (weights \lit{0.25}, \lit{1}, \lit{4}) changes
unseen-pair CRPS; routing through the base ($\text{arm}\to\text{base}\to\text{arm}'$) beats the
direct route in two thirds of unseen cells (\lit{6} seeds); a \lit{36}-mer mappability input
lowers CRPS in the lowest-mappability tenth of bins on read-length and mapping-filter arms,
beside a \lit{5}~Mb shifted-mappability control. After a one-seed smoke test (a short trial run), and before the
full runs, the design was amended: scrambled twins were added, a collapsed
consistency loss was replaced by an agreement loss between routes, and empty top strata were
redefined. The joint probability of meeting every check was \powJoint{}\% to \powPivot{}\%.

\section{Results and discussion}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Which settings act, and where.} Whole-read resampling sets the floor: at
\lit{3.75}M reads per half the narrow \lit{1}~kb shape floor is \shLowNar{}, not
\shLowUnitNar{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b). Peak height moves beyond this floor in \nPhAbove{} of
\nPh{} setting rows, shape in only \nShAbove{} of \nSh{}. Signal level explains \lvlFifteenLo{}
to \lvlFifteenHi{} of the per-bin disagreement; regional features add at most \featMax{}. Depth
acts uniformly (residual \depArm{} against its null \depNull{}); the mapping filter and read
length act in low-mappability bins (feature gain \mapqZeroLo{} to \mapqZeroHi{} and
\cropFeatLo{} to \cropFeatHi{}; Fig.~\ref{fig:results}c).

\paragraph{Trained pairs: the covariates carry the information.} From base to arm, designs B to
D close \gcBDLo{} to \gcBDHi{} of the gap in every mark class (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}a; seed
range at most \gcBDnoiseHi{} of the gap), above \lit{2}$\times$ the seed range in \nBDgain{} of
\nBDcells{} cells. In narrow marks, design B scores \wModel{} (\wModelOS{} shape + \wModelSE{}
scale error), one-warp \wWarp{} (\wWarpOS{} + \wWarpSE{}) and the oracle \wOra{} (\wOraOS{} +
\wOraSE{}); \lit{2}$\times$ the seed range is \wSeed{}. The affine design A shows no gain
(\gcALo{} to \gcAHi{}): a monotone curve gives the main step, and more capacity adds little. For
paired-end, the model beats one-warp by \peGainLo{} to \peGainHi{} in \nPeHistSig{} of
\nPeHist{} histone cells (\lit{2}$\times$ seed range \peSeedLo{} to \peSeedHi{}). A depth offset helps only A, in scale error (DNase \oldA{} $\pm$
\oldAw{} to \offA{} $\pm$ \offAw{}; Table~\ref{tab:goals}). From predicted counts, the peak
caller's formula plus a learned correction gives $p$ best (log CRPS \hBest{} $\pm$ \hBestW{}, \hBestOS{} + \hBestSE{}).

\paragraph{Unseen pairs: the correction does not transfer.} The relative count gain over the
twin falls from \relCntTr{} on trained pairs to \relCntSame{} on unseen pairs. The real arms obey
the depth law within \lawData\%, yet every design meets it on trained pairs (\lawTrAllLo{} to
\lawTrAllHi{}, median $|\log_{\lit{2}}|$ error) and misses it on unseen pairs (\lawNeverAllLo{} to
\lawNeverAllHi{}; Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}a). Removing the pseudocount, a Poisson mean loss,
wider networks and an encoder-decoder do not close the miss. Chaining two trained maps through
the base does (\lawViaAllLo{} to \lawViaAllHi{}). The miss is a failure to transfer, not a lack
of capacity.

\paragraph{Arm-to-arm training teaches scale, not distribution.} One added arm-to-arm pair per
track closes \cOneGap{} of the depth-law gap to the chained map (bar \lit{0.5}), but CRPS gets
worse: \crArm{} (\crArmOS{} + \crArmSE{}, seed range \crArmS{}) against \crRef{} (\crRefOS{} +
\crRefSE{}, \crRefS{}) direct and \crVia{} (\crViaOS{} + \crViaSE{}, \crViaS{}) chained, a gap
closed of \cFourGap{}. Training on every pair reaches the chained map (\crArmTwo{}, \crArmTwoOS{}
+ \crArmTwoSE{}, \crArmTwoS{}; not pre-registered).

\paragraph{Trained CANDI.} Told a \lit{36}~bp read length, CANDI moves its predicted level by
\swLPredLo{} to \swLPredHi{} $\log_{\lit{2}}$ (real \swLRealLo{} to \swLRealHi{}); told
paired-end, by \swPPredLo{} to \swPPredHi{} in histone marks (real \swPRealLo{} to \swPRealHi{})
and \swDPredLo{} to \swDPredHi{} in DNase (real \swDRealLo{}; Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}b). Its
depth control failed, so per-region tracking cannot be read. The PI's reading: CANDI uses
both covariates, but not in the direction the real tracks move.

\paragraph{Joint objective: no change.} At weight \lit{1}, the cross-assay objective moves
unseen-pair CRPS by \jXd{} (bar \jXdBar{}) and denoising by \jDnd{} (bar \jDndBar{}):
neither is statistically significant (Fig.~\ref{fig:final}a). Correction alone scores \jAlone{}
(\jAloneOS{} + \jAloneSE{}, seed range \jAloneS{}); cross-assay \jX{} (\jXOS{} + \jXSE{},
\jXS{}). At weight \lit{4}, cross-assay nearly clears (\jXfourd{} against \jXfourdBar{}),
mostly in scale error (\jXfourdSE{}). The scrambled twins are worse on unseen pairs by
\jXtw{} and \jDntw{}, but the planned twin check on trained pairs cannot fail, because the twin is
scored on its own training labels. Reading: against the claim, weak evidence.

\paragraph{Pivot through the base: no general gain.} Routing beats the direct route beyond the
bar in \nCellWin{} of \nCells{} unseen cells (\nCellNeed{} needed; \nCrossWin{} of \nCross{}
cross-setting cells) and loses in \nCellWorse{}. Pooled, it is lower in \nSeedPos{} of
\nSeedAll{} seeds (gain \pGain{} against bar \pGainBar{}): direct \pDir{} (\pDirOS{} + \pDirSE{},
seed range \pDirS{}), chained \pCh{} (\pChOS{} + \pChSE{}, \pChS{}; Fig.~\ref{fig:final}b). The
chained route follows the depth law (\pLawCh{}, seed range \pLawChS{}; bar \lit{0.16}). On six
seeds the trained-pair control missed its band (\nOneGap{} against \nOneBar{}); the PI counted
it on the three seeds shared with the reference model. Unplanned, the agreement-loss model's direct route scores
\pAg{} (\pAgOS{} + \pAgSE{}, \pAgS{}), \pAgGain{} against bar \pAgBar{}; this is exploratory.
Reading: against, moderate evidence.

\paragraph{Mappability input: a local gain.} In the lowest-mappability tenth of bins the
mappability channel lowers CRPS on all \nMapLPass{} of \nMapTracks{} tracks for read length
\lit{36} (\mMapL{}, \mMapLOS{} + \mMapLSE{}, seed range \mMapLS{}, against \mAlL{}, \mAlLOS{} +
\mAlLSE{}, \mAlLS{}) and for mapping filter \lit{0} (\mMapQ{} against \mAlQ{};
Fig.~\ref{fig:final}c). It beats shifted mappability (\sMapQ{} against bar \sMapQBar{}), the gain
is local (genome-wide \gMap{} against \gAl{}), and a DNA-sequence input gives about a quarter of it. The
depth arm, meant to stay unchanged, improved by \mDepthChg{} (seed standard deviation \mDepthSD{}),
so this no-change control failed. Reading: supports, weak evidence.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

On trained pairs the covariates carry the information: for depth they close most of the gap
to the oracle, and for run type they beat a covariate-free map. On unseen pairs the gain
shrinks and no direct map follows the depth law. Neither a joint objective nor routing through
a shared base changed this; a mappability input helps where reads map poorly. These are
readings: a pre-registered pass of the transfer test on more tracks would settle the question
(Table~\ref{tab:goals}).

\paragraph{Limitations.} Only \lit{7} tracks and \lit{3} to \lit{6} seeds; the final checks
had a joint probability of \powJoint{}\% to \powPivot{}\% of passing if true; the twin check of
the joint objective could not fail; the depth oracle was redefined, and the run-type bar set, after their runs; run type
and control settings have no valid ceiling.

\paragraph{Next steps for the mini-CANDI prototype, without ranking.} The design rulings keep
count space only and depth as a learned probe, and defer costly reprocessing.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A first prototype} with depth and a mappability channel, gated by the twin, the
seed range and the unseen-pair depth law. Testbed scale (training \costTrLo{} to \costTrHi{} min
per arm); teaches whether correction survives inside an imputer.
\item \textbf{A pre-registered test of the agreement loss.} One arm, \lit{6} seeds; teaches
whether the exploratory lead is real.
\item \textbf{Run-type size on more tracks}, from single-end reprocessed challenge tracks. CPU
analysis, if the PI opens that panel; teaches how large the challenge's failure knob is per assay.
\item \textbf{New counterfactual pairs for CANDI's corpus.} Read-level settings need the
pipeline rerun (median \costFastq{}~h per product); teaches whether real pairs fix transfer.
\end{itemize}
