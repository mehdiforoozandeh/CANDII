## Methods

### Data

Every paragraph and table in this section is a draft by the agent, from files that the run recorded. Each number names its source file. Source files are under `cruxvault/results/`; the index of every number is `cruxvault/results/t309/sources.md`.

==**What we built.** The dataset is called `eic_v1`. It holds the tracks of the ENCODE Imputation Challenge (EIC), i.e. a public benchmark of 363 non-control tracks: 267 training, 45 validation and 51 blind tracks (`cruxvault/results/t273/AG2_LEAKAGE.md`). We rebuilt every track from the reads of its published ENCODE experiment. We did not reuse the challenge's processed files. Seven training DNase-seq tracks had a challenge file from another experiment. We rebuilt these seven from the published experiment's own FASTQs (`cruxvault/results/t170/build_plan.md`). The build list has 1,217 rows in four priorities (104, 292, 99 and 722 rows). `eic_v1` holds priorities 1 to 3, i.e. the validation tracks, the training tracks and the designed counterfactual views (`cruxvault/results/t170/check.txt`).==

==**Pipelines.** Each assay ran the standard ENCODE-DCC pipeline of that assay. Histone ChIP-seq and its controls ran chip-seq-pipeline2 v2.2.2. DNase-seq ran dnase-seq-pipeline v3.0.0. ATAC-seq ran atac-seq-pipeline v2.2.3 (`cruxvault/results/t183/fastq_cost.tsv`, column `pipeline`). The portal does not serve 129 FASTQs. These cover 46 experiments of six Roadmap tissue cells completely (6 validation tracks, 31 training tracks and 9 controls). For these 46 we started from ENCODE's redacted unfiltered GRCh38 alignments, i.e. BAM files with read positions but no read sequence. They get every alignment-level view and no FASTQ-level view (`cruxvault/results/t170/build_plan.md`). Controls come from each ChIP experiment's ENCODE control list: 75 control experiments, of which 63 control a training experiment (`cruxvault/results/t170/build_plan.md`).==

==**Views and halves.** A view is one way to process the reads of a track. The base view pools all reads. Three FASTQ-level views imitate other sequencing runs: `se` keeps mate 1 of a paired-end library, and `crop36` and `crop50` cut reads to 36 and 50 bp. Five alignment-level views change processing: three depth thinnings (`depth0.50`, `depth0.25`, `depth0.125`), no duplicate removal (`nodedup`), and no mapping-quality filter (`mapq0`, not for ATAC-seq) (`cruxvault/results/t170/withheld_design.md`). Each view has two pseudoreplicate halves, `pr1` and `pr2`, i.e. a random read-level split of the view into two disjoint halves. All 43 paired-end training experiments get the `se` view (`cruxvault/results/t170/withheld_design.md`). Among training experiments, `crop36` exists for 3 ATAC-seq, 14 broad-histone and 12 narrow-histone experiments, and `crop50` for 3, 14 and 10. DNase-seq has no crop view, because its training reads are 36 bp or shorter (`cruxvault/results/t170/run_type_coverage.tsv`).==

The 78 blind rows are the 7 counterfactual testbed tracks, for evaluation only. It also wrote 15,123 pairs, of which 102 are never-trained designed validation pairs. The `tables_sha256` is `a068a867...b135`. The dataset check printed `check: PASS` with every rule at 0 (`cruxvault/results/t183/DATASET_EIC_V1.md`). Table M1 below counts the tracks per assay (`cruxvault/results/t309/dataset_table.tsv`).==

==**What is absent, and why.** The 21 absent tracks of priorities 1 to 3 were all queued or running at the freeze, and none failed verification. Their causes are as follows (`cruxvault/results/t183/DATASET_EIC_V1.md`, `cruxvault/results/t183/TAIL.md`). Seventeen tracks were slow in DNase-seq alignment: paired-end `bwa sampe` often needs more than one day, with up to 984.9 M reads per row. Two tracks ran out of memory in the Picard step of the pipeline. One paired-end library had no adapter record on the portal. One H3K9ac track hit a read-name format that breaks mate pairing. The tail is still running. Later rows go into update versions of the dataset, and no result of iterations 1 to 3 uses them. Three consequences follow for these results. First, DNase-seq is not imputation-scorable, because only 1 validation track has halves and the rule needs 3. Second, H3K9ac reaches 3 validation targets only through one replicate view, which has no signal-space score. Third, 22 of the 42 validation targets have no accessibility-only item, because their cell has no accessibility training track (`cruxvault/results/t183/DATASET_EIC_V1.md`). HAP-1 has no track in `eic_v1`.==

==**Cost.** PI ruling 3 asked for the alignment cost of the uniform base. Of the 376 experiments of priorities 1 to 3, 330 ran a FASTQ alignment; the other 46 are the redacted-alignment rows. The 330 used 4,511 CPU-h in all, with a median of 6.89 CPU-h per experiment. Slurm allocated 19,894 CPU-h, so the jobs used 4,511 / 19,894 = 23% of what they held. The CPU-h per million reads was 0.198 for chip-seq-pipeline2, 0.136 for dnase-seq-pipeline and 0.310 for atac-seq-pipeline (`cruxvault/results/t183/DATASET_EIC_V1.md`, recomputed from `cruxvault/results/t183/fastq_cost.tsv`). The finished rows ran from 2026-11-11 02:35 to 2026-11-13 08:14 UTC, about 54 h of wall time. The table build itself used 4.32 CPU-h. The working storage on Nibi `/scratch` was 240 GB of measurements, 944 GB of intermediate files and 772 GB of staged FASTQs. The frozen measurements are archived as 55 tar files of 242 GB (`cruxvault/results/t183/compute_and_storage.tsv`).==

==**Covariates and their limits.** The read-derived QC columns are sparse: 16.2% to 19.6% of training rows have NSC, RSC, NRF or PBC values, and no row has FRiP (`cruxvault/results/t183/qc_coverage.tsv`). Of the 249 training tracks present, 73.1% have reads of 36 bp or shorter, and all 7 blind testbed tracks are HiSeq 4000 runs with reads of 76 bp or longer (`cruxvault/results/t183/imbalance.tsv`). Run type has two meanings, and we quote both. As processed, 229 of the 249 training tracks are single-end alignments, 12 are paired-end and 8 are mixed (`cruxvault/results/t183/imbalance.tsv`, which reads the `run_type` column of the `eic_v1` tables). As sequenced, the catalog FASTQs of the same 249 tracks are all single-end for 193, all paired-end for 11 and mixed for 14; the other 31 are redacted-alignment tracks, which ENCODE aligned single-end (`cruxvault/results/t309/sources.md`, section Run type). The two counts differ for two reasons. First, a ChIP-seq experiment that mixes single-end and paired-end runs is aligned single-end from mate 1, as ENCODE4 does (`long-horizon notepad.md`, agent call of 2026-11-11). Second, the redacted alignments are single-end. Over all 267 training experiments of the ledger, 43 have paired-end reads, i.e. 16.1% (`cruxvault/results/t170/run_type_coverage.tsv`). Of the 21 training experiments whose FASTQs are all paired-end, 10 are in the absent tail (`cruxvault/results/t309/sources.md`, section Run type). The blind set is 100% paired-end, 100% HiSeq 4000 and 94% from one lab (`cruxvault/results/t170/withheld_design.md`). The designed views imitate only two real shifts, paired-end to single-end and long to short reads. They cannot imitate platform, lab or the reverse directions (`cruxvault/results/t170/withheld_design.md`).==

### Evaluation

The figure of the protocol is `cruxvault/results/t309/protocol_figure.svg`.

==**Split by tracks.** We use the EIC's own split (PI ruling 2). Models train on training tracks. Every score and every selection uses validation tracks. The blind tracks are read once, at the end (`cruxvault/INSTRUCTIONS/peeling_the_onion/long-horizon notepad.md`, ruling 2).==

==**Regions.** The evaluation set is frozen with sha256 `b5f7a4cf...0ce8` on the tables `a068a867...` (`cruxvault/results/t185/SUBMITTED.md`). It scores 256 regions of 16,384 bins each. A bin is 25 bp (`tools/onion_eval/ag2_peaks.py`, `BIN_BP`), so a region is 409,600 bp and the 256 regions hold 104.9 Mb. That is 256 x 16,384 / 113,251,272 = 3.7% of the valid bins of the genome mask (`cruxvault/results/t292/STRATA_REAL.md`). The regions are drawn at random with seed 0, and each has a valid-bin fraction of at least 0.9. A second, disjoint draw of 16 fit regions holds the bins on which the baselines fit their parameters (`cruxvault/results/t185/SUBMITTED.md`). By the training-peak frequency of their tiles, 1 region is in the class 0 to 0.05, 64 in 0.05 to 0.2, 124 in 0.2 to 0.5 and 67 above 0.5. No region is quiescent (`cruxvault/results/t292/STRATA_REAL.md`).==

==**Items and regimes.** An item is one validation target and its inputs. The set has 63 imputation items. In the leave-one-assay-out regime (43 items), the inputs are the base views of the same cell's training tracks of every other assay. In the accessibility-only regime (20 items), the only input is the cell's DNase-seq or ATAC-seq training track (`cruxvault/results/t185/SUBMITTED.md`; `tools/onion_eval/evalset.py`). No validation track is ever an input. An assay is imputation-scorable when it has at least 3 validation tracks with halves from at least 3 cells and at least 5 training tracks, and at least 2 validation tracks need a never-trained pair that is not a pure depth thinning (`cruxvault/results/t183/DATASET_EIC_V1.md`). Nine assays pass: H3K27ac, H3K27me3, H3K36me3, H3K4me1, H3K4me2, H3K4me3, H3K79me2, H3K9ac and H3K9me3. H3K9ac passes only through its replicate fallback, so a strict count is 8 (`cruxvault/results/t185/SUBMITTED.md`).==

==**Pairs.** A correction pair asks the model to turn a source view of a track into a target view of the same track. The set has 1,480 correction pairs over 11 correction-scorable assays, i.e. the 9 above plus H2AFZ and H4K20me1 (`cruxvault/results/t185/SUBMITTED.md`). A scored pair reads its source from the `pr1` half and its target from the `pr2` half, so the two sides share no read (`cruxvault/results/t170/withheld_design.md`). Headline metric 4 counts only never-trained pairs that are not pure depth thinnings. A never-trained pair is one of 18 directed view combinations that training never samples (for example `nodedup` to `mapq0`). Each validation track gets 2, 4 or 6 such pairs (38, 4 and 3 tracks) (`cruxvault/results/t170/withheld_design.md`, `cruxvault/results/t170/check.txt`). The set also has 3,134 replicate pairs, which measure the variance between replicates for metric 7 and for the variance layers (`cruxvault/results/t185/SUBMITTED.md`).==

==**The seven headline metrics** (`cruxvault/INSTRUCTIONS/peeling_the_onion/onion eval protocol.md`, section headline-metric-set). Each is quoted per assay as a macro average, with its seed spread.==
1. ==Recovered share of specific variance above the noise floor: 1 - (E_sp - N) / (V_sp - N), where E_sp is the cell-type-specific error, V_sp the specific truth variance and N the pseudoreplicate noise floor. It has two spaces: counts for selection, and the challenge's signal space for comparison with EIC entries (PI ruling 13).==
2. ==r_spec: the correlation between the truth and the prediction after a smooth function of the training average at each position is removed from each (`onion eval protocol.md`, section r-spec-specificity-correlation).==
3. ==Count CRPS skill against the per-position average: CRPS(average) - CRPS(model), with each CRPS split into its oracle-scaled and scale-error parts. CRPS is the continuous ranked probability score, i.e. an error of a full predictive distribution.==
4. ==Gap closed against depth-only scaling on never-trained pairs: (CRPS_depth-only - CRPS_model) / (CRPS_depth-only - CRPS_oracle). The oracle is one half of the mean absolute difference between the two read halves of the target view, i.e. the expected CRPS of the true sampling distribution (PI ruling 47; `cruxvault/results/gap_closed_floor/GAP_FLOOR.md`). In composite version 3 (PI ruling 56, 2026-11-16) the lead term is this value on the 18 never-trained crop and crop + single-end pairs that have no duplicate-keeping side (6 assays: H3K36me3, H3K4me2, H3K4me3, H3K79me2, H3K9ac, H3K9me3; scorer key `correction_gap_closed_oracle_no_nodedup_side`). The reason: on 84 of the 102 headline pairs one side keeps duplicates, so source and target can share DNA fragments (`cruxvault/results/correction_metric_review/REVIEW.md`). Beside it we report the correction-only term, 1 - [CRPS_model(A pr1 to B pr2) - CRPS_model(B pr1 to B pr2)] / (CRPS_depth-only - CRPS_half), i.e. the model's loss against its own same-view score, scaled by the covariate cost of a raw copy of the source half. It needs one more prediction pass per run (the same-view pass, scores in `p16_sv` and `p256_sv`), and it is 1 for a model that ignores its input, so it is read together with gap closed (`REVIEW.md`, section 3; `cruxvault/results/final_round/FINAL_ROUND_EQUAL.md`). Superseded: the first definition used the half-against-half floor as the top of the scale (PI ruling 6).==
5. ==Depth-law error on never-trained pairs: the median of the absolute log2 ratio of the predicted total scale to the true depth ratio.==
6. ==PIT deviation, pooled and in the worst stratum: the mean absolute gap between the average non-randomized PIT curve and the diagonal on a 21-point grid. PIT is the probability integral transform, i.e. the predicted CDF at the observed count.==
7. ==Predicted over replicate variance: the predicted Negative Binomial variance divided by the variance between replicates, per replicate rung.==

==**Baselines.** Three baselines need no training (`tools/onion_eval/baselines.py`). The marginal Negative Binomial (NB) is one constant NB per assay. The per-position average is the mean rate over the training cells that measured the assay, leaving the target's cell out, scaled to the target's depth. Depth-only scaling multiplies the source counts by the depth ratio; it serves pairs only. The pseudoreplicate floor is the reference of metric 4, not a competitor. A model output is never blended with a baseline (decision D10). On the validation tracks, AlphaGenome 2 is an external comparison only. It trained on 32 validation tracks in 21 cells, and 12 validation tracks in 10 cells are unseen by both it and us (`cruxvault/results/t273/ag2_fair_sets.tsv`). Its outputs are never an input, a target or a selection signal.==

==**Composite and seed spread.** Each goal has one lead term on a 0 to 1 scale: metric 1 for imputation, metric 4 for correction, and 1 - PIT(arm) / PIT(marginal NB) for uncertainty. The composite is 0.5 imputation + 0.25 correction + 0.25 uncertainty, with each term clipped to [0, 1] (decision D13; PI ruling 18; `tools/onion_eval/select_rule.py`). The seed spread is the maximum minus the minimum across seeds, and a comparison uses the larger spread of its two arms (PI ruling 7). An arm beats the incumbent when its composite is higher by more than 2 seed spreads over at least 3 seeds and no goal drops by more than 2 of that goal's own spreads. Otherwise the simpler arm stays. The rule refuses to compare scores whose evaluation-set or table hashes differ, and it refuses a seed spread of exactly 0 (`cruxvault/INSTRUCTIONS/peeling_the_onion/long-horizon notepad.md`).==

==**Composite versions.** Version 1 used the half-against-half floor as the top of the correction scale; the key `selection.composite` in `scores.json` still holds it and is not quoted. Version 2 (from ship 2) used the oracle floor on all 102 headline pairs (PI ruling 47). Version 3 (2026-11-16, PI ruling 56) uses the oracle floor on the 18 pairs with no duplicate-keeping side; it is the default of `select_rule.py`, and its verdicts are written to `settlements_v3.tsv`. Every composite in Results 9 and later is version 3; Results 4 to 7 are version 2 (`cruxvault/results/correction_metric_review/REVIEW.md`, addendum of 2026-11-16).==

==**Pair regions.** Correction terms are scored on all 256 regions. The check of 16 against 256 pair regions failed its pre-written rule at 8, 16 and 32 regions (Fir job 4534658, Fir `/project/def-maxwl/mforooz/ONION/pair_check/e1_v3/PAIR_REGIONS_V3.md`), so 16-region scores stay screening scores. Imputation and calibration always use all 256 regions.==

==**Leak rules.** The dataset check enforces these with a count of 0 each: no validation or blind measurement in a training pair, no withheld combination in a training pair, no blind row readable in training or validation mode, and every testbed row flagged evaluation-only (`cruxvault/results/t183/DATASET_EIC_V1.md`). The evaluation set has 0 items, inputs and pairs on the blind split (`cruxvault/results/t185/SUBMITTED.md`). The NB sizes of the baselines and of the floor are fit on the fit regions, which never hold a scored bin (`tools/onion_eval/evalset.py`). Read-derived QC never enters the output-side covariates, because it would leak the target (decision D6). Training windows may overlap evaluation regions (PI ruling 39); the run measures the gap instead.==

==**The single blind pass.** The blind set is scored once, at the end, from its own evaluation set `eval/eic_v2/evalset_blind.json`. Each blind pass is written to a ledger, `BLIND_PASSES.tsv`, so a second look is visible and needs a stated reason. The final model for the EIC comparison trains on training plus validation tracks, as the challenge entrants did (PI ruling 21). Iterations 1 to 3 read no blind track.==

==**Two scoring tiers.** A settled score runs each pair on all 256 regions, or on the smallest of 8, 16 or 32 nested regions that passes a check written before any model was scored (`cruxvault/results/pair_region_check/PAIR_CHECK_PREREGISTRATION.md`). A screening score runs pairs on 16 nested regions, and items keep all 256 regions. Each table states its tier. No selection is settled from a screening score. The baselines of iteration 1 are settled scores.==

==**Telescoping check of the layers.** Of 2,925 groups of assay, region stratum and scale band, 390 add up exactly, 2,145 are not expected to add up because their rungs hold different cells, and 390 are empty; none is unexplained (`cruxvault/results/t305/LAYERS_REAL.md`).==

==**Region selection for training (interim).** At a training window of 1,024 bins and sampling tiles of 2,048 bins, only 23 of 51,394 tiles hold no training peak, so lists stratified by peak class sample almost uniformly (effective sample size per draw 0.86 to 0.94). The selection arms therefore use a continuous tilt of chosen strength (`cruxvault/results/t292/STRATA_REAL.md`; `onion region budget and selection.md`, interim finding 1).==

### Model

Source: `tools/minicandi/registry.py` (`RUNGS`), `tools/minicandi/configs/rung<k>/base.json`, and `run.json` of each run on Nibi (`$O/runs/<run>/seed<k>/run.json`; `/scratch/mforooz/onion/mtb/out/runjson.tsv`).

==The model family is miniCANDI, a ladder of six architectures with about 1.0 M parameters each (993,599 to 1,042,514). Rung 1 is pointwise, i.e. it sees one 25 bp bin at a time with no sequence context. Rung 2 is a convolutional autoencoder, rung 3 a convolutional autoencoder with attention, rung 4 a U-Net, rung 5 an axial model and rung 6 a Perceiver. Every rung reads a window of 1,024 bins and trains on 80,000 windows. The output per assay and bin is a Negative Binomial with mean = depth x 2^eta, i.e. the network predicts a depth-free log rate eta and the target depth enters as a fixed offset. The network also reads log2 depth as a soft covariate, together with the other three experimental covariates, through FiLM layers (feature-wise scaling and shifting) on the input side and before the head. The NB size is a per-bin output. Recorded training compute is 7.8 x 10^15 FLOPs for rung 1 (on GPU) and 1.3 x 10^14 to 1.8 x 10^15 FLOPs for rungs 2 to 6 (on CPU).==

==The arms change one thing against a base. The rung-1 arms switch off the DNA input, scramble the four covariates, inject the covariates by concatenation or as tokens instead of FiLM, mark a missing assay with a learned mask token, train on 24,565 instead of 80,000 windows to match a smaller compute budget, or scramble all inputs. The rung-4 arms scramble the covariates, train on a region set without the evaluation regions, or hide the depth covariate from the network while the mean keeps the depth offset (the hard-offset arm, PI ruling 48). The selection arms train rung 4 on 20,000 windows drawn by one selection rule, against uniform tiling.==

### Results 1: the variance layers on real data

Source: `cruxvault/results/t305/LAYERS_REAL.md` and `layers_by_assay.tsv`, `layers_by_stratum.tsv` of the same folder (the rerun with the pseudoreplicate pairs; the first run is not used). No model is involved.

==We measured three variance layers per assay as differences of pair values on log1p counts in 25 bp bins: the sampling layer (two pseudoreplicate halves of one library, 778 pairs), the replicate layer above sampling (isogenic replicates minus sampling, 219 pairs), and the cell-type layer (pairs of different cell types minus replicates, 2,911 pairs). Each pair is thinned to the assay's common depth, which is now the depth of one pseudoreplicate half, and each increment carries a 95% cluster-bootstrap interval over cells.==

==The sampling layer is the largest layer of every assay at 25 bp. Over all regions it is 76% (DNase-seq) to 97% (ChIP-seq control) of the three layers. The replicate layer above sampling (sequencing, library and biological replicate together) is small: 0.1% to 6% of the three layers, and about 1% for most marks. Its interval excludes 0 for 8 assays (H2AFZ, H3K27ac, H3K27me3, H3K4me1, H3K4me2, H3K4me3, H3K79me2, H3K9ac); for example H3K4me3 0.00040 [0.00015, 0.00065] against a sampling layer of 0.0222 [0.0186, 0.0257]. So at 25 bp, two pseudoreplicate halves of one experiment differ almost as much as two biological replicates.==

==The cell-type layer (mixed with donor and experiment) is 23% for DNase-seq, 20% for H3K27ac and H3K4me3, and 10% for the broad marks H3K27me3, H3K36me3 and H3K9me3; its interval excludes 0 for 8 assays, and the control has none (-0.00007 [-0.0034, 0.0033]). It is the largest layer at the broad scale band (cell-type share 0.60 to 0.91) and leads at the domain band for the narrow marks and DNase-seq. Among region classes it is largest in promoters (DNase-seq 0.51, H3K4me3 0.38, H3K27ac 0.34).==

==The layers hypothesis held in part. For H3K27me3, H3K36me3 and H3K9me3 the sampling layer is 0.88 to 0.89 of the three layers at 25 bp, as expected. The expectation that the cell-type layer leads for the narrow marks and accessibility fails at 25 bp over all regions (sampling 0.76 to 0.85, cell type at most 0.23) and holds at the broad scale and in promoters for DNase-seq.==

==Caveats. The shares are at the depth of one pseudoreplicate half, and on log1p counts the sampling share falls as depth grows, so at full depth its share is smaller. The sampling layer itself does not shrink with depth at these counts: in a simulation (`cruxvault/results/eta_space/THEORY.md`, section 4), the log1p sampling variance rose with depth up to about 5 reads per bin (0.0023 at 0.005 reads per bin, 0.205 at 5) and fell above it (0.032 at 100), and the data sit at about 0.02 to 0.05 reads per bin. The replicate layer pools three sources, and the cell-type layer holds donor and lab, because the sequencing, technical, anisogenic and other-experiment rungs have no pair. DNase-seq rests on 33 pseudoreplicate, 8 isogenic and 136 other-cell-type pairs, because 18 training DNase-seq tracks are absent from `eic_v1`.==

### Results 2: the training-free baselines

Source: `cruxvault/results/t307/BASELINES.md` and its tables (`baselines_headline.tsv`, `correction_headroom.tsv`, `calibration_baselines.tsv`, `noise_floor_by_assay.tsv`, `validity_checks.tsv`). Tier: settled (pairs on all 256 regions). Interval: 95% cluster-bootstrap over cells.

==The scorer passed its validity checks with exact equality on both tiers: depth-only scaling scored a gap closed of exactly 0 and the half-against-half floor exactly 1 on every assay and knob, and the uncertainty term of the marginal negative binomial was exactly 0. A second computation from the per-pair records reproduced three scorer macros to every printed digit.==

==The four training-free baselines set the reference on the three goals. For imputation, the position average, i.e. the per-position mean of the training cells scaled to the target depth, recovered 0.119 [-0.156, 0.194] of the cell-type-specific variance above the noise floor in counts space; the noise floor itself was 58% to 85% of that specific variance per assay at 25 bp. Its count CRPS was 0.795 (0.775 oracle-scaled + 0.019 scale error), and the marginal negative binomial was worse by 0.216 [0.129, 0.309] (count CRPS 1.011 = 0.845 + 0.166). Signal-space scores were undefined for every baseline, because no baseline predicts a fragment length.==

==For covariate correction, depth-only scaling had a mean CRPS of 0.561 per bin on the 102 never-trained pairs, against 0.519 for the half-against-half floor, a gap of 0.042 [0.019, 0.081] (macro over the 11 scorable assays: 0.572 = 0.561 + 0.011 against 0.533 = 0.523 + 0.010). Over all 1,480 correction pairs the gap depended on the direction of the depth change: it was negative when the target was shallower than the source (depth-only scaling won on every such pure-depth pair) and positive when the target was deeper (0.081 to 0.234 across the depth knobs), because scaling up multiplies the source's sampling noise. The position average, which ignores the source track, went below the floor on the never-trained pairs (macro correction CRPS 0.523 = 0.513 + 0.010) and closed 2.86 [1.85, 6.59] of the gap. The floor is therefore not a lower bound on the correction CRPS, and the reference a model must beat is the position average.==

==How to rescale this metric was open when this was written; the PI then ruled the oracle floor as its 1 (PI ruling 47; Methods, metric 4). The scorer computes the gap closed per assay as a ratio of mean CRPS values and then averages over the 11 assays; the per-assay denominators (depth-only minus floor) are 0.007 to 0.162, so an assay with a small denominator gives a large ratio (H3K27me3 9.63). Until the ruling, every correction score is also quoted as raw CRPS with its split (per-assay values: `cruxvault/results/t307/src/baselines/<name>/seed0/scores.json`).==

==For uncertainty, the position average was closer to calibration than the marginal negative binomial (pooled PIT deviation 0.050 against 0.060; worst stratum 0.074 against 0.185). Both baselines predicted at least the variance between two pseudoreplicate halves in every scorable assay (ratio 1.21 to 16.97 for the position average), and the position average predicted more variance than separates two cell types in 6 of 7 assays. The upper bound of the variance ratio needs a second experiment of the same cell, which `eic_v1` lacks. On these baselines, scoring the pair jobs on 16 instead of 256 regions left the imputation and uncertainty metrics unchanged and moved the correction gap closed by 0.49, inside its interval.==

==The headroom expectation (the gap is largest for the depth and run-type knobs and near 0 for pure subset pairs) is not supported as written: the run-type knob rests on 1 cell, pooled over both directions the largest gaps are crop+mapq (0.116 [0.064, 0.170]) and depth+nodedup (0.107 [0.061, 0.164]), and the subset pairs have a gap of 0.035 [0.021, 0.055], which mixes -0.011 on shallower targets and 0.081 on deeper ones.==

### Results 3: AlphaGenome 2, the training-track audit and the board against the baselines

Source: `cruxvault/results/t273/AG2_LEAKAGE.md` (audit), `cruxvault/results/t308/SUBMITTED.md` (pack and board status), `cruxvault/results/ag2/AG2_FACTS.md` (what the model predicts). Label: interim, dataset `eic_v1`, validation tracks.

==AlphaGenome 2 takes 1 Mb of DNA and a named condition and predicts every track of that condition; measured tracks of the cell are not an input. It predicts histone ChIP-seq as fold change over control in 128 bp bins and accessibility as a normalized insertion signal at 1 bp, so we compare it with our models in the signal space. Its released model trained on every observed track, and no accession list is public, so we audited each challenge track at the level of condition and mark. Of the 45 validation tracks, AlphaGenome 2 trained on the same condition and mark for 32, on a sibling condition for 1, and never saw 12; of the 51 blind tracks it trained on 36 and never saw 15, all of them H3K27me3 or H3K9me3. Every validation and blind track is queryable.==

==In `eic_v1` the headline regime holds 43 validation targets, because two validation DNase-seq tracks are absent. The packed predictions therefore hold a fair set of 11 tracks in 9 cells that neither AlphaGenome 2 nor our models trained on, and a trained-on set of 31 tracks in 20 cells; the sibling-condition track is set aside. The two sets are scored and reported apart, and only the fair set can carry a claim about unseen tracks.==

Board source: `cruxvault/results/t308/AG2_VS_BASELINES.md`, with `ag2_board_val.tsv`, `ag2_contrasts_val.tsv` and `ag2_per_target_val.tsv` of the same folder. Interval: 95% cluster bootstrap over cells, 2,000 resamples.

==We scored AlphaGenome 2 and the training-free baselines on the same validation tracks, regions and truth, in the asinh signal space at 25 bp. Each prediction got the same per-track isotonic recalibration to the truth, i.e. a monotone map fitted on the test track itself. The primary signal metric is the recalibrated r_spec, i.e. the correlation with the truth after both have the mean of the training tracks removed. The peak metric is the area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC) against ENCODE peak tiles of 500 bp. On the fair set of 11 tracks in 9 cells, AlphaGenome 2 had a recalibrated r_spec of 0.438, against 0.222 for the position average and 0.121 for the marginal Negative Binomial. The position average minus AlphaGenome 2 was -0.216 [-0.297, -0.123], and the position average was ahead on 0 of 11 tracks. Every other baseline was also behind, with an interval that excludes 0, on recalibrated r_spec and on peak AUPRC. On Spearman correlation, the position average minus AlphaGenome 2 was -0.031 [-0.106, +0.064], so that comparison has no call. On the 30 tracks that AlphaGenome 2 trained on, its recalibrated r_spec was 0.614, against a noise floor of 0.585. That result measures the fit of AlphaGenome 2 to its training tracks, so it is not evidence about imputation.==

## Discussion

First draft, on screening scores. Each statement gives its evidence and its limit.

1. ==Sequence context helps both goals at first, and then the goals separate. Rung 2 beats the pointwise rung 1 on specific variance (+0.042, spread 0.030) and on gap closed (+0.102, spread 0.023) by more than the seed spread (composite +0.058). The uncertainty term moved +0.047, within its larger seed range of 0.091, so the gain is not on every headline term (`cruxvault/results/ladder_audit/COVERAGE.md`, "Where a doc disagrees", item 1). Limit: the composite gain is below the 2 seed spreads that the selection rule asks for, and it rests on 3 seeds on 16 pair regions under composite version 2.==
2. ==No architecture leads both imputation and correction. Rung 4 recovers the most specific variance (0.440) and closes the least gap of rungs 1 to 5 (0.298); rungs 2 and 3 close the most gap (0.576 and 0.572). Limit: the cause is open, and the gap-closed term on all headline pairs mixes clean pairs with pairs that can share DNA fragments.==
3. ==Depth must stay an input on the input side of the network, even though the mean already carries depth as an offset. Hiding depth on both sides cost 0.184 of composite at rung 4, and four fifths of that came from calibration (uncertainty term 0.600 to -0.099). The split arms place the need on the input side: hiding depth on the input side only cost 0.062 of composite (0.444 to 0.382, ranges 0.030 and 0.009), and hiding it on the output side only did not hurt (0.455 (0.013), 3 seeds) (`cruxvault/results/views_ablation/VIEWS_ABLATION.md`; `COVERAGE.md`, "Where a doc disagrees", item 2). Limit: one rung, screening tier, composite version 2; whether the NB size or eta carries the needed depth information is not yet measured.==
4. ==Gap closed on all headline pairs can reward a model for the wrong reason. At rung 4, scrambling the covariates raised it by 0.101 and lowered gap closed on the 18 clean pairs by 0.099. Limit: the clean subset holds 18 pairs in 6 assays, and no direct test of fragment sharing has run.==
5. ==In the depth-free eta space, the variance layers above read sampling do not change with depth, so model and layer results can be compared at one reference without thinning. Thinning to 1/2 and 1/4 of the common depth moved the eta-space replicate and cell-type layers by at most 10%, while the log1p cell-type share fell by 8% to 63% (`cruxvault/results/eta_space/LAYERS_ETA.md`). Limit: the eta estimates are weighted toward high-count bins, the replicate layer is not small in eta space (8% to 59% of the sampling-free variance), and no model score is yet read in eta space.==

==Training-region selection by the tilts that ran does not beat uniform tiling at 20,000 windows, so the uniform draw stays for now.==
